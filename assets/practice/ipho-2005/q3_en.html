Problem T3 [10 pts]. Neutrons in a gravitational field

In an experiment at the Institut Laue–Langevin in Grenoble, neutrons traversed a gap between a horizontal crystal mirror M at \(z=0\) and an absorber A at \(z=H\). The mirror reflects elastically, while every neutron touching the absorber is removed. The horizontal speed \(v_x\) is constant from entrance W to detector D. The initial vertical velocity may have either sign. Neglect losses during free flight.

1 5.5 pts Classical motion

1 1.5 pts Find the classical range of \(v_z\) that permits a neutron entering at height \(z\) to reach D. Treat \(L\) as much larger than any other length in the apparatus.

2 1.5 pts Find the minimum classical length \(L_c\) ensuring that neutrons outside this velocity range are absorbed, whatever their entry height. Take \(v_x=10\,\mathrm{m/s}\) and \(H=50\,\mu\mathrm m\).

3 2.5 pts At entry the neutron rate is uniform in both height and vertical velocity, with \(\rho\,dz\,dv_z\) neutrons per unit time in an element \(dz\,dv_z\). Find the classical transmitted count rate \(N_c(H)\).

2 4.5 pts Quantisation of the vertical motion

The experimental transmission rose in steps as the gap height crossed critical values \(H_1,H_2,\ldots\). Use the stated Bohr–Sommerfeld approximation for the vertical motion. Here \(p_z\) is the classical vertical momentum and the integral covers one complete bounce cycle.

\[\oint p_z\,dz=nh,\qquad n=1,2,3,\ldots .\]

4 2.5 pts Determine the turning heights \(H_n\) and vertical energies \(E_n\) using this rule. Calculate \(H_1\) in micrometres and \(E_1\) in electronvolts.

Take \(H_1<H<H_2\). A long gap transmits only the lowest quantum state even though the incident neutron velocities have a continuous distribution. In a short gap the flight time limits the energy resolution and can wash out the first step.

5 2 pts Use the energy–time uncertainty relation to estimate the minimum flight time \(t_q\) and gap length \(L_q\) needed to observe the first sharp step. Use \(v_x=10\,\mathrm{m/s}\).

The following constants and antiderivative are supplied.

\[h=6.63\times10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s},\qquad c=3.00\times10^8\,\mathrm{m/s},\qquad e=1.60\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm C .\]
\[M=1.67\times10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg},\qquad g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.\]
\[\int\sqrt{1-x}\,dx=-\frac23(1-x)^{3/2}+C .\]
