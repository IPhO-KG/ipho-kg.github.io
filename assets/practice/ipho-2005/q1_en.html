Problem T1 [10 pts]. An ill-fated satellite

A communications satellite of mass \(m\) moves in a circular equatorial geosynchronous orbit of radius \(r_0\). Its period is one sidereal day. Its engine normally changes the speed along the tangent, but here the impulse will be radial. Use

\[R_T=6.37\times10^6\,\mathrm m,\quad g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2},\quad T_0=24.0\,\mathrm h.\]

1 1.5 pts Circular orbit

1.1 0.3 pts Calculate the orbital radius \(r_0\) measured from the centre of Earth.

1.2 0.4 pts Express the speed \(v_0\) through \(g,R_T,r_0\) and evaluate it.

1.3 0.8 pts Find the angular momentum magnitude \(L_0\) and the total mechanical energy \(E_0\) in terms of \(v_0,m,g,R_T\). Take the potential energy to vanish at infinity.

2 3.8 pts A radial impulse and an ellipse

The engine fires by mistake in the operational orbit. The impulse points towards Earth’s centre and adds an inward velocity component \(\Delta v\). The burn is much shorter than any orbital timescale. Write \(\beta=\Delta v/v_0\), with \(0<\beta<1\) in this part.

2.1 0.9 pts Express the new orbit’s semi-latus rectum \(l\) and eccentricity \(\varepsilon\) in terms of \(r_0,\beta\). Conic-section relations are supplied below.

2.2 1 pt What angle \(\alpha\) does the major axis make with the radius vector at the instant of the impulse?

2.3 1.2 pts Express the perigee and apogee distances from Earth’s centre, \(r_{\min}\) and \(r_{\max}\), using \(r_0,\beta\). Evaluate both for \(\beta=1/4\).

2.4 0.7 pts Find the period \(T\) in terms of \(T_0,\beta\) and evaluate it for \(\beta=1/4\).

3 1.5 pts Threshold for escape

3.1 0.5 pts Find the least value \(\beta_{\mathrm{esc}}\) for which the satellite can escape Earth.

3.2 1 pt At this limiting impulse, express the closest approach \(r'_{\min}\) to Earth’s centre in terms of \(r_0\).

4 3.2 pts Hyperbolic escape

Take \(\beta>\beta_{\mathrm{esc}}\) and consider the free orbit in Earth’s field shown below.

4.1 1 pt Determine the asymptotic speed \(v_\infty\) in terms of \(v_0,\beta\).

4.2 1 pt Find \(b\), the perpendicular distance from Earth’s centre to the outgoing asymptote, using \(r_0,\beta\).

4.3 1.2 pts Find the angle \(\phi\) marked in F-2 in terms of \(\beta\). Evaluate it at \(\beta=\tfrac32\beta_{\mathrm{esc}}\).

An inverse-square central force gives a conic orbit. For \(m\ll M\) the attracting body of mass \(M\) is at one focus. In the angular convention of F-3 the polar radius is related to the semi-latus rectum \(l\) and eccentricity \(\varepsilon\) by

\[r(\theta)=\frac{l}{1-\varepsilon\cos\theta},\qquad l=\frac{L^2}{GMm^2},\qquad \varepsilon=\sqrt{1+\frac{2EL^2}{G^2M^2m^3}} .\]

Here \(L\) is the angular momentum magnitude about the focus and \(E\) the mechanical energy with zero at infinity. The orbit is elliptic for \(0\leq\varepsilon<1\) (circular at zero), parabolic at \(\varepsilon=1\) and hyperbolic for \(\varepsilon>1\).
