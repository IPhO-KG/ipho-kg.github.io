Problem T1 [10 points]. A three-body problem and LISA

Two bodies of masses \(M\) and \(m\) attract one another gravitationally. Their circular orbits about the common centre of mass \(O\) have respective radii \(R\) and \(r\). Let \(G\) denote the gravitational constant.

T1-1.1 [1.5 pts]. Find the angular velocity \(\omega_0\) of the line joining the two bodies in terms of \(G,M,m,R,r\).

A third body of negligible mass \(\mu\) follows a circular orbit about \(O\) in the same plane. It stays fixed relative to both massive bodies, and the three positions are not collinear.

T1-1.2 [3.5 pts]. Express the following distances in terms of \(R,r\): 1.2.1 from \(\mu\) to \(M\), 1.2.2 from \(\mu\) to \(m\), and 1.2.3 from \(\mu\) to the centre of mass \(O\).
T1-1.3 [3.2 pts]. Now set \(M=m\) and give \(\mu\) a small radial perturbation along \(O\mu\). Assuming that its angular momentum is conserved, express the angular frequency of small oscillations about its unperturbed position in terms of \(\omega_0\).

In the LISA design considered in this 2011 problem, three identical spacecraft form an equilateral triangle with sides about \(5.0\) million km long. Laser signals exchanged between them measure the small arm-length changes caused by low-frequency gravitational waves.

The constellation follows an Earth-like orbit about the Sun, trailing Earth by \(20^\circ\). Its slightly inclined individual orbits give the triangle one rotation about its own centre per year.
