Problem T1 [10 points]. Dark matter

The galaxy speeds observed in the Coma cluster are too large to be bound by the gravity of the visible matter alone. Consider three ways to infer the missing mass. Treat dark matter as interacting with visible material only through gravity and moving with its host galaxy.

T1-A [5.2 points]. A galaxy cluster

A large population of \(N\) galaxies and their dark matter fills a sphere of radius \(R\) uniformly. The cluster has mass \(M\) and the mean mass per galaxy, including its associated dark matter, is \(m\).

T1-A1 [1 point]. Model the cluster as a continuous mass distribution and find its gravitational potential energy in terms of \(M\) and \(R\).

A Lyman line with terrestrial rest frequency \(f_0\) is observed at \(f_i\) in a type Ia supernova in galaxy \(i\). All speeds are small compared with the speed of light \(c\).

T1-A2 [0.5 points]. Infer the cluster's mean recession speed \(V_{cr}\) from the measured frequencies \(f_1,\ldots,f_N\).

T1-A3 [1.5 points]. The internal velocities are isotropic. Determine the three-dimensional rms speed \(v_{\rm rms}\) relative to the cluster centre using \(N\), \(f_i\) and \(f_0\). Give the mean kinetic energy per galaxy in this frame.

The virial relation for a bound system is \(\langle K\rangle_t=-\gamma\langle U\rangle_t\), where the brackets are long-time averages. Bounded momenta and positions keep \(\Gamma=\sum_i\mathbf p_i\!\cdot\!\mathbf r_i\) finite.

T1-A4 [1.7 points]. Use \(\langle d\Gamma/dt\rangle_t=0\) to derive \(\gamma\) for gravity. It may help to start with just a few galaxies.

T1-A5 [0.5 points]. Find the dark mass of the cluster in terms of \(N\), mean visible mass \(m_g\) per galaxy, \(R\) and \(v_{\rm rms}\). The dark and luminous components have the same rms speed.

T1-B [2.8 points]. The galactic rotation curve

Consider a spherical galaxy of visible radius \(R_g\). Stars have constant number density \(n\) inside the edge. Outside, a few tracer stars remain but their density is negligible. Treat the stars as point masses of mean mass \(m_s\) on circular orbits.

T1-B1 [0.8 points]. Assuming the stars supply all the mass, find and sketch the orbital speed \(v(r)\) on either side of \(R_g\).

The measured rotation curve has the shape shown. Approximate it by a straight line from zero to \(v_0\) at \(R_g\), followed by a constant \(v_0\) outside.

T1-B2 [0.5 points]. Find the total mass \(m_R\) enclosed within \(R_g\) from \(v_0\) and \(R_g\).

T1-B3 [1.5 points]. Determine the dark matter density in both regions, \(r<R_g\) and \(r\ge R_g\), using \(r\), \(R_g\), \(v_0\), \(n\) and \(m_s\).

T1-C [2 points]. Interstellar gas

A young spherical galaxy is made predominantly of gas and dark matter. Neglect stars. The gas consists of particles of mass \(m_p\) with radial number density \(n(r)\) and temperature \(T(r)\), and is in hydrostatic equilibrium.

T1-C1 [0.5 points]. Let \(m'(r)\) denote the total gas and dark mass enclosed at radius \(r\). Express \(dP/dr\) in terms of \(m'(r)\), \(r\) and \(n(r)\).

T1-C2 [0.5 points]. For an ideal gas infer \(m'(r)\) from the profiles \(n(r)\) and \(T(r)\) and their derivatives.

T1-C3 [1 point]. Take \(T=T_0\) and \(n(r)=\alpha/[r(\beta+r)^2]\) for constants \(\alpha,\beta\). Find the dark matter density profile.
