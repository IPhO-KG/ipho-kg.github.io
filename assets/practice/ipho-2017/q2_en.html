Problem T2 [10 points]. Earthquake, volcano and tsunami

T2-A [1.3 points]. The eruption of Merapi

The October 2010 eruption of Java's Merapi produced a 12 km ash column. Model the interaction of water with magma in two stages. Magma of mass \(m_m\) at \(T_m\) first reaches thermal equilibrium with water of mass \(m_w\) initially at \(T_w\), at nearly fixed volume. Neglect latent heats. The mixture then expands.

T2-A1 [0.5 points]. Find the equilibrium temperature after mixing, given the specific heats at constant volume \(c_{Vw}\) and \(c_{Vm}\).

T2-A2 [0.3 points]. Find the equilibrium pressure at this stage, treating the mixture as an ideal gas with molar volume \(v_e\).

T2-A3 [0.5 points]. The gas speed during expansion depends on pressure \(p\), mass \(m\) and volume \(V\) in the vent. Determine this dependence up to a dimensionless factor \(\kappa\).

T2-B [5.7 points]. The Yogyakarta earthquake

The magnitude \(M_w=6.4\) Yogyakarta earthquake began at 22:54:00 UTC (05:54 local time). Its source on the Opak fault was 15 km deep. Stations YOGI and DNP are 22.5 and 500 km from the epicentre. The seismograms show vertical ground velocity. Longitudinal P waves precede transverse S waves; surface waves travel at about \(0.6v_P\). Beneath the 30 km crust lies the mantle. Neglect Earth's curvature.

T2-B1 [0.5 points]. Read the YOGI seismogram to determine the P-wave speed in the crust.

T2-B2 [0.6 points]. Find the travel times to DNP for the direct P wave and for the P wave reflected at the crust–mantle boundary.

T2-B3 [1.2 points]. Each of the two layers is homogeneous and the mantle is faster. A critically refracted ray travels along the interface while sending energy back into the crust. Use Snell's law and the DNP record to find the mantle P-wave speed.

Now let the speed increase continuously with depth \(z\) as \(v(z)=v_0+az\). Source and receiver lie effectively at the surface. The local angle to the vertical is \(\theta(z)\) and the conserved ray parameter is \(p=\sin\theta(z)/v(z)\).

T2-B4 [1.4 points]. Find the surface distance from source to receiver in terms of \(p\), \(v_0\) and \(a\).

T2-B5 [1 point]. Express the travel time \(T\) as an integral over depth.

T2-B6 [1 point]. Approximate the DNP travel time using three layers. Their speeds are \(6.65\), \(6.97\) and \(6.99\) km/s and their thicknesses are \(6.0\), \(9.0\) and \(15\) km respectively. Use \(p=0.143\) s/km.

T2-C [3 points]. The Java tsunami

The 17 July 2006 earthquake lifted part of the ocean floor. Approximate the water raised above sea level by a rectangular block of height \(h\) and base area \(\lambda L/2\), with \(L\gg\lambda\). Water density is \(\rho\), and ocean depth \(d\) is small compared with wavelength \(\lambda\).

T2-C1 [0.5 points]. Find the gravitational energy of the raised water relative to the original surface.

T2-C2 [1.2 points]. Derive the propagation speed of the tsunami up to a dimensionless constant.

T2-C3 [1.3 points]. Use energy conservation to find how the amplitude varies over a gently changing depth if it equals \(A_0\) at depth \(d_0\).
