Problem T3 [10 points]. Cosmic inflation

On scales above 100 Mpc the universe is approximately homogeneous and isotropic. The recession of distant galaxies suggests global expansion, described here by a scale factor \(a(t)\).

T3-A [5 points]. Expansion of the universe

Follow a sphere expanding with the material inside a much larger region of uniform density. Its radius is \(R(t)=a(t)R_s\), where \(R_s\) is a fixed reference radius. The first Friedmann equation reads

\[\left(\frac{\dot a}{a}\right)^2=A_1\rho-\frac{kc^2}{R_s^2a^2}.\]

T3-A1 [1.3 points]. Find \(A_1\) by applying Newtonian gravity to a test mass on the sphere. The constant \(k\) is dimensionless and \(c\) is the speed of light.

In the relativistic description \(\rho c^2\) is total energy density excluding gravitational potential energy. Apply the first law to an adiabatically expanding sphere at pressure \(p\).

T3-A2 [0.9 points]. Determine \(A_2\) in \(\dot\rho+A_2(\rho+p/c^2)\dot a/a=0\).

Write \(p=w\rho c^2\) with constant \(w\) and define \(H=\dot a/a\). A subscript zero denotes the present epoch and \(a_0=1\). The wavelength of a photon grows in proportion to \(a\).

T3-A3 [1.2 points]. Find \(w\) for radiation, nonrelativistic matter and a component with constant energy density.

T3-A4 [1.2 points]. For a flat universe (\(k=0\)), find \(a(t)\) in each of the three cases. Impose \(a(0)=0\) in the first two and \(a(t_0)=1\) in the last.

Positive, zero and negative spatial curvature correspond to \(k=+1,0,-1\). Define \(\Omega=\rho/\rho_c\), where \(\rho_c\) is the density of a flat universe with the same \(H\), obtained by setting \(k=0\) in the first Friedmann equation.

T3-A5 [0.1 points]. Express \(k\) using \(\Omega\), \(H\), \(a\) and today's sphere radius \(R_0=R_s\).

T3-A6 [0.3 points]. Give the range of \(\Omega\) for each of the three values of \(k\).

T3-B [1.9 points]. Why introduce inflation?

The cosmic microwave background indicates that today's \(\Omega\) is close to unity. Consider the fate of a small initial curvature under different expansion laws.

T3-B1 [0.5 points]. Find how \(\Omega-1\) varies with time during radiation domination and during matter domination.

T3-B2 [0.3 points]. Find this dependence during domination by constant energy density, assuming a small departure from flatness.

T3-B3 [0.9 points]. Show that the inflationary regime has negative pressure, accelerated expansion and a decreasing comoving Hubble radius \((aH)^{-1}\).

T3-B4 [0.2 points]. Show that a decreasing \((aH)^{-1}\) is equivalent to \(\epsilon<1\) for \(\epsilon=-\dot H/H^2\).

Inflation ends at \(\epsilon=1\). Define the e-fold variable by \(dN=d\ln a=H\,dt\) and \(N=0\) at the end of inflation.

T3-C [1.7 points]. A homogeneous scalar field

Assume a uniform inflaton field \(\phi(t)\) with potential \(V(\phi)\). A prime denotes differentiation with respect to \(\phi\), and \(M_{\rm pl}\) is the reduced Planck mass. The field obeys

\[\ddot\phi+3H\dot\phi=-V',\qquad H^2=\frac{\dot\phi^2/2+V}{3M_{\rm pl}^2}.\]

T3-C1 [1.7 points]. In slow roll, assume \(\dot\phi^2/2\ll V\) and negligible \(\ddot\phi\) compared with \(3H\dot\phi\). Express \(\epsilon\), \(\eta_V=\delta+\epsilon\) and \(dN/d\phi\) using \(V\), \(V'\) and \(V''\), where \(\delta=-\ddot\phi/(H\dot\phi)\).

T3-D [1.4 points]. A power-law potential

Let \(V=\Lambda^4(\phi/M_{\rm pl})^n\) for integer \(n\). At the start of the interval use \(r=16\epsilon\) and \(n_s=1+2\eta_V-6\epsilon\). Use the observational bounds specified in the problem: \(n_s=0.968\pm0.006\), \(r<0.12\).

T3-D1 [0.5 points]. Find the field value \(\phi_{\rm end}\) at the end of inflation.

T3-D2 [0.9 points]. Express \(r\) and \(n_s\) in terms of \(N\) and \(n\). Compare integer powers with the given bounds 60 e-folds before the end. Keep track of the sign convention for \(dN\).
