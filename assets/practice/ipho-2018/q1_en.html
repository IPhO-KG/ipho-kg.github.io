Question T1 [10 points]. The gravitational wave GW150914

LIGO's first gravitational-wave detection, GW150914, came from two black holes in nearly circular orbits. Infer the properties of this binary from the changing frequency of the signal.

T1-A [3 points]. Newtonian orbits

Two isolated stars of masses \(M_1\) and \(M_2\) move nonrelativistically. Their positions obey \(M_1\mathbf r_1+M_2\mathbf r_2=0\) in the centre-of-mass frame. Use \(G=6.67\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/kg^2}\).

T1-A1 [1 point]. Write the acceleration as \(\ddot{\mathbf r}_1=-\alpha\mathbf r_1/r_1^n\), where \(r_1=\lvert\mathbf r_1\rvert\). Determine the positive integer \(n\) and the coefficient \(\alpha(G,M_1,M_2)\).

T1-A2 [1 point]. Now take circular orbits of angular speed \(\Omega\) and separation \(L=r_1+r_2\). Set \(M=M_1+M_2\) and \(\mu=M_1M_2/M\). Find \(A\) in the total energy \(E=A(\mu,\Omega,L)-GM\mu/L\).

T1-A3 [1 point]. Express \(E\) as \(\beta GM\mu/L\) and determine \(\beta\).

T1-B [7 points]. Energy loss through gravitational waves

A nonrelativistic circular binary radiates gravitational waves at twice its orbital frequency. Its leading-order luminosity is given by the quadrupole formula below, with \(c=3.0\times10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}\).

\[\mathcal P=\frac{G}{5c^5}\sum_{i=1}^3\sum_{j=1}^3\left(\frac{\mathrm d^3Q_{ij}}{\mathrm dt^3}\right)^2.\]

The orbit lies in the \(xy\) plane. Star \(A\) has coordinates \((x_A,y_A,0)\). The following quadrupole components can be nonzero, with each sum taken over the two stars.

\[Q_{11}=\frac13\sum_A M_A(2x_A^2-y_A^2),\qquad Q_{22}=\frac13\sum_A M_A(2y_A^2-x_A^2).\]
\[Q_{33}=-\frac13\sum_A M_A(x_A^2+y_A^2),\qquad Q_{12}=Q_{21}=\sum_A M_Ax_Ay_A.\]

T1-B1 [1 point]. For circular orbits write \(Q_{ii}=\tfrac12\mu L^2(a_i+b_i\cos kt)\) and \(Q_{ij}=\tfrac12\mu L^2c_{ij}\sin kt\) for \(i\ne j\). Determine \(k\), the triples \((a_i)\) and \((b_i)\), and the constants \(c_{ij}\).

T1-B2 [1 point]. Calculate the radiated power and determine \(\xi\) in \(\mathcal P=\xi G\mu^2L^4\Omega^6/c^5\). You may use \(\xi=6.4\) in subsequent parts.

T1-B3 [1 point]. Radiation causes the orbit to shrink through a sequence of nearly circular configurations. Show that \((\mathrm d\Omega/\mathrm dt)^3=(3\xi)^3\Omega^{11}(GM_c)^5/c^{15}\) and express the chirp mass \(M_c\) in terms of \(M\) and \(\mu\).

T1-B4 [2 points]. Relate \(\Omega\) to the wave frequency \(f_{\mathrm{GW}}\). For \(\dot F=\chi F^a\) with constant \(\chi\) and \(a\ne1\), you may use \(F^{1-a}=\chi(1-a)(t-t_0)\). Derive the following relation and determine \(p\).

\[f_{\mathrm{GW}}^{-8/3}=8\pi^{8/3}\xi\left(\frac{GM_c}{c^3}\right)^{2/3+p}(t_0-t)^{2-p}.\]

LIGO has two perpendicular arms of length 4 km each. The graph shows the fractional change in an arm's length on 14 September 2015. This response follows the wave linearly. Points \(A,B,C,D\) occur at \(0\), \(0.009\), \(0.034\) and \(0.040\,\mathrm s\). The peak after \(D\) approximately marks the merger.

T1-B5 [1 point]. Estimate the wave frequency at the midpoint of each interval \(AB\) and \(CD\). Assume equal component masses and extrapolate B4 to merger to estimate \(M_c\) and the total mass in units of \(M_\odot=2\times10^{30}\,\mathrm{kg}\).

T1-B6 [1 point]. Estimate the separation at \(t_{CD}\), an upper bound \(R_{\max}\) on the radius of either object, and the ratio \(R_\odot/R_{\max}\) for \(R_\odot=7\times10^5\,\mathrm{km}\). Express the orbital speed of each component at this instant as a fraction of \(c\).
