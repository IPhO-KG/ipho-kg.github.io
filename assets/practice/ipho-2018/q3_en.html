Normal atmospheric pressure is \(1.013\times10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}=760\,\mathrm{mmHg}\).

T3-A [4.5 points]. The physics of blood flow

For steady laminar flow of an incompressible fluid through a rigid cylindrical tube, the pressure drop \(\Delta P\) and volume flow rate \(Q\) obey Poiseuille’s law. For length \(\ell\), radius \(r\) and viscosity \(\eta\),

\[\Delta P=RQ,\qquad R=\frac{8\ell\eta}{\pi r^4}.\]

The hydraulic resistance \(R\) plays the part of electrical resistance if current represents flow and voltage represents pressure drop. This model estimates blood flow while neglecting pulsation and wall elasticity.

Consider a symmetric network of arterioles. Each vessel bifurcates into two identical vessels of the next level. Levels run from \(0\) to \(N-1\). The radii and lengths scale as \(r_{i+1}=2^{-1/3}r_i\) and \(\ell_{i+1}=2^{-1/3}\ell_i\).

T3-A1 [1.3 points]. Express the flow \(Q_i\) through one level-\(i\) vessel using \(N,\eta,r_0,\ell_0\) and the difference \(\Delta P=P_0-P_{\mathrm{cap}}\) between the inlet pressure and the pressure of the capillary bed.

For a network with \(N=6\), \(r_0=6.0\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm m\), \(\ell_0=2.0\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm m\) and \(\eta=3.5\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}s^{-1}}\), find \(Q_0\) in millilitres per hour. The inlet and outlet pressures are \(55\,\mathrm{mmHg}\) and \(30\,\mathrm{mmHg}\).

Pressure pulses are appreciable in large vessels and much weaker in small ones. An expanding vessel stores fluid, modelled by a capacitance \(C\). The fluid’s inertia provides an inductance \(L\). For a vessel of wall thickness \(h\) and wall Young’s modulus \(E\),

\[C=\frac{3\ell\pi r^3}{2Eh},\qquad L=\frac{9\ell\rho}{4\pi r^2}.\]

T3-B [5.5 points]. Tumour growth

An inextensible membrane confines tissue to a sphere of fixed radius \(R\) and volume \(V\). Initially it is filled with normal tissue at atmospheric pressure. A spherical tumour of radius \(R_{\mathrm{T}}\) then grows at the centre, compressing the surrounding tissue.

Let \(p\) be the common pressure above atmospheric pressure. The normal tissue and tumour share the same unstressed density \(\rho_0\). Their densities under compression are

\[\rho_{\mathrm{N}}=\rho_0\left(1+\frac{p}{K_{\mathrm{N}}}\right),\qquad \rho_{\mathrm{T}}=\rho_0\left(1+\frac{p}{K_{\mathrm{T}}}\right),\]

where \(K_{\mathrm{N}},K_{\mathrm{T}}\) are their respective bulk moduli.

T3-B1 [1 point]. The normal tissue mass \(M_{\mathrm{N}}\) remains fixed. Express the tumour volume fraction \(v=V_{\mathrm{T}}/V\) in terms of \(\mu=M_{\mathrm{T}}/M_{\mathrm{N}}\) and \(\kappa=K_{\mathrm{N}}/K_{\mathrm{T}}\).

In the thermal model, near-infrared-absorbing nanotubes are uniformly distributed throughout the tumour. They release thermal power \(\mathcal P\) per unit volume, with no heat sources outside the tumour. Thermal conductivity \(k\) is constant and the same in all regions. The outward heat flux density is \(-k\,dT/dr\). Far away, the temperature is \(T_b=37\,^\circ\mathrm C\).

T3-B2 [1.7 points]. In the steady state, express the central tumour temperature in terms of \(\mathcal P,k,T_b\) and \(R_{\mathrm{T}}\).

T3-B3 [0.5 points]. The tumour radius is \(5.0\,\mathrm{cm}\) and \(k=0.60\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-1}K^{-1}}\). Find the minimum heating power per unit volume needed to bring the entire tumour above \(43.0\,^\circ\mathrm C\).

The tumour is supplied by a network of the type in A1. For \(p>P_{\mathrm{cap}}\) only the narrowest vessels, at level \(N-1\), undergo a positive radius reduction \(\delta r\). At the critical pressure \(p_c\) the reduction is \(\delta r_c\) and the vessels collapse. Use the model relation

\[\frac{p}{P_{\mathrm{cap}}}-1=\left(\frac{p_c}{P_{\mathrm{cap}}}-1\right)\left(2-\frac{\delta r}{\delta r_c}\right)\frac{\delta r}{\delta r_c}.\]
