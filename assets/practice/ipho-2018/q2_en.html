Question T2 [10 points]. Where is the neutrino?

A proton collision at the Large Hadron Collider produces charged and neutral particles. Quarks give rise to jets of hadrons. ATLAS measures charged particles’ momenta from the curvature of their tracks in a magnetic field. A track with low transverse momentum can curl up inside the detector. A neutrino escapes without leaving a track.

The ATLAS solenoid supplies a uniform field \(B=2.00\,\mathrm T\) in the inner detector. Use \(m=9.11\times10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}\), \(e=1.60\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm C\), \(c=3.00\times10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}\) and \(\epsilon_0=8.85\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{F/m}\).

T2-A2 [0.5 points]. The inner region is a cylinder of radius \(R=1.1\,\mathrm m\). An electron is produced on its axis. Find the minimum momentum, in \(\mathrm{MeV}/c\), needed to reach the cylindrical wall. Use relativistic momentum.

For a charge \(e\) accelerated perpendicular to its velocity, the synchrotron radiation power is

\[P=\frac{e^2a^2\gamma^4}{6\pi\epsilon_0 c^3},\qquad \gamma=\frac1{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}.\]

T2-A3 [1 point]. In the ultrarelativistic limit \(v\simeq c\), write the power as \(P=\xi e^4E^2B^2/(\epsilon_0m^kc^n)\), where \(E\) is the total particle energy. Determine the coefficient \(\xi\) and the integer exponents \(n,k\).

T2-A4 [1 point]. The electron starts with energy \(E_0\). In the ultrarelativistic limit its energy obeys \(E(t)=E_0/(1+\alpha E_0t)\). Determine \(\alpha\) in terms of \(e,c,B,\epsilon_0,m\).

T2-B [6 points]. Finding the neutrino

At 00:21:24 GMT+1 on 4 June 2015, ATLAS recorded a proton collision producing a \(t\bar t\) pair. The top decays through \(t\to bW^+\) and \(W^+\to\mu^+\nu\). The antitop gives \(\bar bW^-\), followed by a quark–antiquark pair from the \(W^-\). Treat particles and jets other than \(t,\bar t,W^\pm\) as massless.

The beam axis is \(z\). The total final-state momentum vanishes in the \(xy\) plane, so missing transverse momentum gives the neutrino’s two transverse components. The longitudinal total need not vanish. The table lists the top decay products’ momentum components in \(\mathrm{GeV}/c\).
