Question T1 [10 points]. Characterizing soil colloids

Micrometre-sized soil particles suspended in water behave as colloids. Their random motion and their response to a field reveal measurable properties.

T1-A [1.6 points]. Motion of a colloidal particle

The one-dimensional velocity obeys

\[M\dot v=-\gamma v+F(t)+F_{\mathrm{ext}}(t).\]

The particle has mass \(M\), drag coefficient \(\gamma\), and experiences random molecular impacts represented by \(F\). Set the external force \(F_{\mathrm{ext}}\) to zero in part A.

T1-A1 [0.8 points]. A particle initially at rest receives an impulse \(I_0\) at \(t_0\) and coasts without further impacts. Its subsequent speed is \(v_0e^{-(t-t_0)/\tau}\). Determine \(v_0\) and \(\tau\); the symbol \(\tau\) may be used below.
T1-A2 [0.8 points]. At each time \(t_i\) an impulse \(I_i\) acts on the particle. For \(v(0)=0\) and \(t>0\) give the velocity and state which collision times enter your expression.

T1-B [1.8 points]. A discrete model of motion

Divide time into intervals \(\delta\gg\tau\). The velocity is constant at \(v_n\) throughout \((n-1)\delta<t\le n\delta\). Its ensemble mean is zero, \(\langle v_n^2\rangle=C\), and \(\langle v_nv_m\rangle=0\) for \(n\ne m\). The parameter \(C\) may depend on \(\delta\). At \(t=N\delta\) write \(\Delta x(t)=x(t)-x(0)\).

T1-B1 [1 point]. Find the first and second moments of displacement in terms of \(C,\delta,t\).
T1-B2 [0.8 points]. In the continuum limit \(\delta\to0\) the measured second moment is finite and nonzero. Determine the exponents in \(C\propto\delta^\alpha\) and \(\langle(\Delta x)^2\rangle\propto t^\beta\).

T1-C [2.7 points]. Electrophoresis

A narrow channel of cross section \(A\) contains suspended particles of mass \(M\) and positive charge \(Q\). Neglect gravity, interparticle interactions and additional wall, fluid and ion effects. A field \(E\) along \(x\) establishes a concentration gradient in the number density \(n(x)\). After the field is removed diffusion smooths it, with number flux per unit area and time

\[J_D=-D\,dn/dx.\]
T1-C1 [0.5 points]. Half the particles move at \(+v\) and half at \(-v\). Over time \(\delta\) right-moving particles within \(v\delta\) of \(x_0\) cross that section. Linearize \(n(x)\) about \(x_0\) and calculate their crossing rate \(N_+(x_0)\) per unit area.
T1-C2 [0.7 points]. Use the corresponding leftward crossing rate \(N_-\) to find \(J_D=\langle N_+-N_-\rangle\) with \(\langle v^2\rangle=C\). Compare with the diffusion law to express \(D\) through \(C,\delta\), then write the mean square displacement using \(D,t\).

At temperature \(T\) the osmotic pressure is \(\Pi=nRT/N_A=nkT\), with \(k=R/N_A\). Keep the field on, so that the nonuniform concentration is stationary. Consider a slice of thickness \(\Delta x\).

T1-C3 [0.5 points]. Obtain \(dn/dx\) from the force balance on the particles in the slice, in terms of \(n,T,Q,E,k\).
T1-C4 [0.5 points]. In the original equation take \(F_{\mathrm{ext}}=QE\) and \(\langle F(t)\rangle=0\). Starting at zero mean velocity, find \(\langle v(t)\rangle\) and its long-time limit \(u\).
T1-C5 [0.5 points]. Drift contributes a number flux \(J_Q=nu\). Use \(J_Q+J_D=0\) in equilibrium to obtain \(D\) in terms of \(k,\gamma,T\).

T1-D2 [0.8 points]. Give each interval the velocity \(u+v_n\), where \(v_n\) has the same statistics as in B. Find the second moment, its early- and late-time power laws and the crossover time \(t_*\). Sketch the result on logarithmic axes and indicate \(t_*\).

Model a swimming microbe of radius \(a\) in one dimension. Its propulsion speed is either \(+u_0\) or \(-u_0\), with an independent choice of sign every \(\delta_0\). Ordinary Brownian diffusion with coefficient \(D\) acts at the same time.

T1-D3 [0.6 points]. The data show three successive power-law regimes. Express the leading mean square displacement in each regime using \(D,u_0,\delta_0,t\).

T1-E [1.5 points]. Coagulation in water treatment

Two suspended particles interact by van der Waals attraction and repulsion of their electric double layers. With distance \(d\) between the surfaces and ion charges \(\pm q\), use

\[U(d)=-\frac A d+\frac{B\epsilon(kT)^2}{q^2}e^{-d/\lambda},\qquad \lambda=\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon kT}{2N_Aq^2 c}}.\]

Here \(A,B\) are positive, \(\epsilon\) is the permittivity of water and \(c\) the molar concentration of salt.

T1-E1 [1.5 points]. What is the threshold NaCl concentration for coagulation of a pair of particles? Ignore thermal fluctuations and assume that the terminal velocity associated with \(-U'(d)\) is reached immediately.
