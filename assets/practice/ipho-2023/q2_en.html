Question T2 [10 points]. Neutron stars

Begin with the stability of an atomic nucleus and extend the model to a neutron star. The travel times of pulses from a binary system provide an independent mass measurement.

T2-A [2.5 points]. Nuclear mass and stability

For a nucleus of \(Z\) protons and \(N\) neutrons let \(A=Z+N\) and take the nucleon mass as \(m_N\). Define the binding energy by

\[m(Z,N)c^2=Am_Nc^2-B(Z,N).\]

Retain volume, surface, electrostatic and symmetry terms:

\[B=a_VA-a_S A^{2/3}-a_C\frac{Z^2}{A^{1/3}}-a_{\mathrm{sym}}\frac{(N-Z)^2}{A}.\]

Use \(a_V=15.8\), \(a_S=17.8\), \(a_C=0.711\) and \(a_{\mathrm{sym}}=23.7\), all in MeV.

T2-A1 [0.9 points]. Find the mass number that maximizes \(B/A\) along the sequence \(Z=N\).
T2-A2 [0.9 points]. For \(A=197\), find the atomic number \(Z^*\) at which the model gives the largest binding energy.
T2-A3 [0.7 points]. Test whether two equal daughter nuclei have less total rest mass than their parent. Reduce \(m(Z,N)>2m(Z/2,N/2)\) to \(Z^2/A>C_{\mathrm{fission}}a_S/a_C\) and find the dimensionless coefficient to two significant figures.

T2-B [1.5 points]. A star as a giant nucleus

T2-B1 [1.5 points]. Model an all-neutron nucleus with \(M=m_NA\) and \(R=R_0A^{1/3}\), \(R_0=1.1\,\mathrm{fm}\). Add \(B_{\mathrm{grav}}=3GM^2/(5R)=a_{\mathrm{grav}}A^{5/3}\) to its binding energy. Evaluate \(a_{\mathrm{grav}}\) in MeV, then omit the surface term and estimate the threshold \(A_c\) for positive binding, both to one significant figure. Use \(G=\hbar c/M_P^2\), \(M_Pc^2=1.22\times10^{22}\,\mathrm{MeV}\), \(m_Nc^2=939\,\mathrm{MeV}\) and \(\hbar c=197\,\mathrm{MeV\,fm}\).

T2-C [6 points]. A pulsar in a binary system

Pulses from neutron star N travel to Earth E past a white dwarf W. Take \(x\) along the light ray toward Earth. The closest point on the line to W has \(x=0\), the impact parameter is \(d\) and Earth lies at \(x_E>0\).

T2-C1 [1 point]. Clock I stands a height \(\Delta h>0\) above clock II in a uniform field \(g\). An identical clock F starts from rest at I, where \(\Delta\tau_F=\Delta\tau_{\mathrm{I}}\). When F passes II, use the velocity of II in F’s freely falling frame and Lorentz time dilation. Relate \(\Delta\tau_{\mathrm{II}}\) to \(\Delta\tau_{\mathrm{I}}\) to first order in \(\Delta\phi/c^2\), with \(\Delta\phi=g\Delta h\).
T2-C2 [1.8 points]. A distant observer assigns coordinate speed \(c_{\mathrm{eff}}\simeq c(1+2\phi/c^2)\), including spatial curvature, with \(\phi(\infty)=0\). Locally the speed is \(c\). Approximate the path by a straight line and find the extra travel time from \(x_N<0\) to \(x_E>0\) caused by mass \(M_{\mathrm{WD}}\), to leading order for \(d/|x_N|,d/x_E,GM_{\mathrm{WD}}/(c^2d)\ll1\). You may use \(\int dx/\sqrt{x^2+d^2}=\ln(x+\sqrt{x^2+d^2})+C\).
T2-C3 [1.8 points]. The stars follow circular orbits about their common centre with separation \(L\). The line of sight makes a small angle \(\varepsilon\) with the orbital plane. The greatest and least delays occur near \(x_N=-L\) and \(x_N=L\). Find their difference to the accuracy used in C2, assuming the gravitational contributions of other bodies cancel.
T2-C4 [0.8 points]. Read the observed delay range from the graph and estimate \(M_{\mathrm{WD}}/M_\odot\) to one significant figure. Use \(L\simeq6\times10^6\,\mathrm{km}\), \(\cos\varepsilon=0.99989\) and \(GM_\odot/c^3\simeq5\,\mu\mathrm s\).
T2-C5 [0.4 points]. Two neutron stars spiral inward as waves carry away energy and angular momentum. At any given circular orbit, neglect relativistic corrections and find \(p\) in \(\omega=\chi R^p\), with \(\chi\) independent of radius and angular frequency.
T2-C6 [0.2 points]. The wave amplitude scales as \(R^2\omega^2\). Select the trace (a)–(d) consistent with the approach to merger.
