Problem T1 [10 points]. Black holes

Use mass \(M\), length \(L\), time \(T\) and temperature \(K\) as base dimensions. For example, a fall through height \(h\) under acceleration \(g\) takes a time of the form \(a\sqrt{h/g}\). Dimensional analysis cannot fix the pure number \(a\). In the first two parts set undetermined dimensionless prefactors to one. Retain factors that subsequently arise from the calculations.

T1-1 [2.3 points]. Fundamental constants

T1-1.1 [0.8 points]. Give the dimensions of Planck's constant \(h\), the speed of light \(c\), the gravitational constant \(G\) and Boltzmann's constant \(k_B\) in terms of \(M,L,T,K\).

A black surface at absolute temperature \(\theta\) emits power \(\sigma\theta^4\) per unit area according to the Stefan–Boltzmann law.

T1-1.2 [0.5 points]. Determine the dimensions of the Stefan–Boltzmann constant \(\sigma\).

T1-1.3 [1 point]. Write \(\sigma=h^\alpha c^\beta G^\gamma k_B^\delta\) up to a dimensionless prefactor. Determine all four exponents by dimensional analysis.

T1-2 [2.1 points]. Horizon and entropy

Consider an uncharged, nonrotating black hole characterized only by its mass \(m\). Its event horizon bounds the region from which even light cannot escape. The horizon area \(A\) depends on \(m\), \(G\) and \(c\).

T1-2.1 [0.8 points]. Use the dimensions of area to find the exponents in \(A=G^\alpha c^\beta m^\gamma\).

Classically the horizon area cannot decrease. Motivated by the analogous thermodynamic property, Bekenstein assigned the hole an entropy \(S=\eta A\).

T1-2.2 [0.2 points]. Use \(dS=\delta Q/\theta\), with exchanged heat \(\delta Q\) and absolute temperature \(\theta\), to find the dimensions of entropy.

T1-2.3 [1.1 points]. Infer \(\eta\) in terms of \(h,c,G,k_B\) using dimensional analysis.

T1-3 [3.2 points]. Hawking radiation

For the remainder of the problem derive the relations from physical laws. You may use your dimensional results from the preceding parts. Hawking's prediction is that a black hole radiates like a black body at temperature \(\theta_H\).

T1-3.1 [0.8 points]. Use thermodynamics and \(E=mc^2\) to obtain \(\theta_H\) in terms of mass and fundamental constants. The hole does no work on its surroundings.

T1-3.2 [0.7 points]. Use the Stefan–Boltzmann law to find the rate of mass change of an isolated hole. Show how it depends on \(\theta_H\), then express it through \(m\) and fundamental constants.

T1-3.3 [1.1 points]. How long does a hole initially of mass \(m\) take to evaporate completely in this model? Denote the time by \(t^*\).

T1-3.4 [0.6 points]. Find the heat capacity of a hole of mass \(m\).

T1-4 [2.4 points]. Equilibrium with background radiation

A hole is immersed in uniform cosmic blackbody radiation at temperature \(\theta_B\). A surface of area \(A\) absorbs power \(\sigma A\theta_B^4\) in this model, while the hole continues to emit.

T1-4.1 [0.8 points]. Express the net rate of mass change through \(m\), \(\theta_B\) and fundamental constants.

T1-4.2 [0.4 points]. For what mass \(m^*\) does the net change vanish? Express it using \(\theta_B\) and fundamental constants.

T1-4.3 [0.2 points]. Use 4.2 to eliminate \(\theta_B\) from 4.1, giving the rate in terms of \(m\), \(m^*\) and constants.

T1-4.4 [0.4 points]. What is the Hawking temperature in thermal equilibrium with the background?

T1-4.5 [0.6 points]. Is this equilibrium stable? Give a mathematical justification.
