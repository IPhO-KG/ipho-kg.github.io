Problem T3 [10 points]. Binary stars

Two stars with small apparent separation may appear as a single source. Their periodic brightness and spectral shifts reveal the motion of each component about the common centre of mass.

T3-1 [2.4 points]. Eclipse photometry

Two stars move on circular orbits at angular frequency \(\omega\). Earth is in their orbital plane, so the components periodically eclipse one another. Star 1 is larger and hotter (\(T_1>T_2\), \(R_1>R_2\)). The graph gives total irradiance \(I\) relative to its maximum \(I_0=4.8\times10^{-9}\) W/m². The minima reach \(0.90 I_0\) and \(0.63 I_0\). Time is in days.

T3-1.1 [0.8 points]. Determine the orbital period in seconds to two significant figures and the angular frequency in radians per second.

Treat each star as a uniformly bright blackbody disc. Its contribution to the observed irradiance is proportional to visible disc area times \(T^4\).

T3-1.2 [1.6 points]. Use the light curve to find \(T_1/T_2\) and \(R_1/R_2\).
