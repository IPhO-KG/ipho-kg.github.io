Problem T2 [10 pts]. The extremum principle

Action, optical travel time and matter-wave phase give three ways to select a path. Throughout this problem neglect gravity and relativistic corrections.

T2-A [1.5 pts]. Mechanical action

On a smooth horizontal plane, the line \(x=x_1\) separates a region of zero potential energy on the left from one of potential energy \(V_0\) on the right. A particle of mass \(m\) travels from \(O=(0,0)\) to \(P=(x_0,y_0)\). Its speeds in the two regions are \(v_1,v_2\) and its angles to the \(x\) axis are \(\theta_1,\theta_2\).

T2-A1 [0.2 pts]. Find \(v_2\) in terms of \(m,v_1,V_0\).

T2-A2 [0.3 pts]. Express \(v_2\) using \(v_1,\theta_1,\theta_2\).

Define the action along a path by \(A=m\int v(s)\,ds\). One lap around a circle of radius \(R\) at speed \(v\), for instance, gives \(2\pi mRv\). At fixed energy the actual path between fixed endpoints makes \(A\) stationary. Within a constant potential it is a straight line.

T2-A3 [1 pt]. Write \(A(\alpha)\) for a crossing at \((x_1,\alpha)\). Apply stationarity to express \(v_1/v_2\) in terms of the coordinates.

T2-B [4 pts]. Fermat’s principle

A horizontal interface separates media of refractive indices \(n_1\) and \(n_2\). The ray makes angles \(i_1,i_2\) to the normal. Fermat’s principle makes the travel time between fixed endpoints stationary.

T2-B1 [0.5 pts]. Use Fermat’s principle to derive the relation between \(\sin i_1\) and \(\sin i_2\).

A horizontal laser beam enters a sugar solution at the origin. Both the concentration and the refractive index decrease upwards. The \(y\) axis points up and the tank floor is at \(-y_0\).

T2-B2 [1.5 pts]. Suppose the index varies only with \(y\), with \(n(0)=n_0\). Apply B1 to obtain the ray slope \(dy/dx\).

T2-B3 [1.2 pts]. For \(n(y)=n_0-ky\) with positive \(n_0,k\), determine \(x(y)\). The following antiderivatives may be used.

\[\int\sec t\,dt=\ln(\sec t+\tan t)+C,\qquad \sec t=\frac{1}{\cos t}.\]
\[\int\frac{du}{\sqrt{u^2-1}}=\ln\!\left(u+\sqrt{u^2-1}\right)+C.\]

T2-B4 [0.8 pts]. Find the coordinate \(x_0\) where the ray hits the floor, taking \(y_0=10.0\,\mathrm{cm}\), \(n_0=1.50\) and \(k=0.050\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}\).

T2-C [1.8 pts]. Action and de Broglie waves

Consider de Broglie waves travelling from \(O\) to \(P\) along all possible paths. Their interference can select the classical trajectory.

T2-C1 [0.6 pts]. On an infinitesimal segment \(\Delta s\), relate the phase increment \(\Delta\varphi\) to the action increment \(\Delta A\) and Planck’s constant.

An opaque screen on the boundary from part A has an opening \(CD\) of width \(d\), small compared with both \(x_1\) and \(x_0-x_1\). The route \(OCP\) is the classical path.

T2-C2 [1.2 pts]. Find the phase difference \(\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{CD}}\) between \(OCP\) and \(ODP\) to first order in \(d\).

T2-D [2.7 pts]. Electron interference

An electron gun at \(O\) sends a collimated beam through slit \(F\) in a partition at \(x=x_1\). The points \(O,F,P\) lie on a straight line at \(\theta=10.0000^\circ\) to the \(x\) axis. The observation screen containing \(P\) is \(L=x_0-x_1=250.00\,\mathrm{mm}\) beyond the partition. The speeds on its two sides are \(v_1=2.0000\times10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}\) and \(v_2=1.9900\times10^7\,\mathrm{m/s}\). Neglect interactions between electrons.

\[m_e=9.109\times10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg},\quad e=1.602\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm C,\quad h=6.626\times10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}.\]

T2-D1 [0.3 pts]. What potential difference \(U_1\) accelerates initially stationary electrons to \(v_1\)?

T2-D2 [0.8 pts]. An identical slit \(G\) is opened \(d=215.00\,\mathrm{nm}\) below \(F\). The waves from the two slits reach \(P\) with a phase difference \(2\pi\beta\). Calculate \(\beta\).

T2-D3 [1.2 pts]. Find the shortest distance from \(P\) to a point on the screen with zero electron detection probability. You may use \(\sin(\theta+\delta)\simeq\sin\theta+\delta\cos\theta\) for small \(\delta\).

T2-D4 [0.4 pts]. The beam has a square cross section \(500\,\mathrm{nm}\times500\,\mathrm{nm}\) and the apparatus is \(2\,\mathrm m\) long. Find the minimum particle flux density \(I_{\min}\) for an average occupancy of at least one electron.
