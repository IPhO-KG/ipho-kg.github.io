Question T1 [10 points]. A water-powered rice mortar

In the hills of northern Vietnam, running water powers a wooden mortar that removes the bran from hulled rice. A pestle is fixed at right angles to the narrow end of a wooden lever. A bucket is carved into its wider end. The lever turns about a horizontal axle. With the lever horizontal, the pestle rests on the rice.

At the start of the cycle the bucket is empty. Water slowly fills it while the lever remains horizontal (a). The pestle lifts and the water shifts towards the far end, increasing the tilt (b). Overflow begins at angle \(\alpha_1\). At \(\beta\) the two gravitational torques balance (c). Inertia carries the lever farther until the bucket empties at \(\alpha_2\) (d). Incoming water drains straight out while the tilt increases to its maximum \(\alpha_0\) (e). The empty lever then swings back and the pestle strikes the rice (f).

A second mode is possible. If the lever is stopped at \(\alpha=\beta\) with the water at the rim, the pestle stays raised. For sufficiently high inflow this state is stable and the mortar ceases to work. Denote the least such mass flow rate by \(\Phi_2\).

The lever and pestle without water have mass \(M=30\,\mathrm{kg}\) and moment of inertia \(I=12\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}\) about axle \(T\). The lever's centre of mass is \(G\). The water has mass \(m\) and centre of mass \(N\). The tilt \(\alpha\) increases as the pestle rises from the horizontal position. Figure 3 gives \(L=0.74\,\mathrm m\), \(a=0.20\,\mathrm m\), \(h=0.12\,\mathrm m\), width \(b=0.15\,\mathrm m\) and wall angle \(30^\circ\). Neglect axle friction and the force of the incoming stream. Treat the water surface as horizontal.

Geometry and balance3 points

The initially horizontal lever starts moving when the mass of water reaches \(m=1.0\,\mathrm{kg}\).

1.1 1 point Find \(TG\), which is horizontal in the initial position.

1.2 0.5 points Find the angle \(\alpha_1\) at which overflow starts and the angle \(\alpha_2\) at which the bucket becomes empty.

1.3 1.5 points Find the angle \(\beta\) where the combined gravitational torque \(\mu\) about \(T\) vanishes and the remaining water mass \(m_1\).

Working cycle3.5 points

The inflow \(\Phi\) is constant and so slow that the water added while the lever moves is negligible. Treat the moment of inertia as constant in this part.

2.1 2 points Sketch \(\mu(\alpha)\) for a cycle. Give its numerical values at \(\alpha=0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2\).

2.2 0.7 points Interpret geometrically the net work \(W_{\mathrm{total}}\) of the torque over a cycle and the work \(W_{\mathrm{pounding}}\) delivered to the rice.

2.3 0.8 points Estimate \(\alpha_0\) and \(W_{\mathrm{pounding}}\) from the graph, neglecting the kinetic energy carried by inflow and outflow. A piecewise-linear graph is sufficient.

Equilibrium with the pestle raised3.5 points

The inflow is constant, and the water entering while the lever moves now matters. In 3.1.1–3.1.3 the water stays at the rim at all times.

3.1.1 0.5 points Sketch \(\mu(\alpha)\) near \(\beta\) and classify the equilibrium.

3.1.2 0.2 points Find the torque to first order in the small displacement \(\Delta\alpha=\alpha-\beta\).

3.1.3 0.8 points Release the lever at a small displacement with zero initial angular velocity. Write its equation of motion, show why the motion is approximately harmonic and find its period \(\tau\).

3.2 1.5 points As the tilt decreases the bucket has to refill. Find the least inflow \(\Phi_1\) in kg/s that keeps the bucket full during harmonic oscillations of amplitude \(1^\circ\).

3.3 0.5 points Suppose water stays at the rim on the return from \(\alpha_2\) to \(\alpha_1\). Using the harmonic approximation, estimate the smallest inflow \(\Phi_2\) at which the pestle ceases to strike the rice.
