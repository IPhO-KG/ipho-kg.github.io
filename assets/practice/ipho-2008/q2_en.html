Question T2 [10 points]. Cherenkov light and a ring-imaging counter

In a transparent medium of refractive index \(n\), light travels at \(c/n\), where \(c\) is the vacuum speed of light. A charged particle with \(v>c/n\) emits radiation at a definite angle to its trajectory. Cherenkov observed the effect in 1934, and Tamm and Frank explained it in 1937. Write \(\beta=v/c\).

\[\cos\theta=\frac{1}{n\beta}.\]

Wavefront1 point

A particle moves uniformly along \(AB\), passing \(A\) at \(t=0\) and reaching \(B\) at \(t=t_1\). A spherical wave travels at \(c/n\) from each point \(C\) that the particle passes. Their envelope at a given time is the wavefront.

1.1 0.5 points Determine the front at \(t_1\). Draw its section in a plane containing the trajectory.

1.2 0.5 points Express the angle \(\varphi\) between a generator and the trajectory in terms of \(n\) and \(\beta\).

Mirror image1.5 points

A beam travels along \(IS\) and crosses a concave spherical mirror at \(S\). Its centre of curvature is \(C\), its focus is \(F\), and its focal length is \(f\). The angle between \(SC\) and \(SI\) is \(\alpha\). Keep only first-order terms in the small ray angles \(\alpha\) and \(\theta\) for the optical construction. This is the geometry of a ring-imaging Cherenkov counter (RICH). The medium traversed by the particles is called the radiator.

2 1.5 points Construct the reflected rays. Explain why they form a ring in the focal plane and find its centre \(O\) and radius \(r\).

Pressure and emission thresholds2.5 points

A beam with momentum \(p=10.0\,\mathrm{GeV}/c\) contains protons, kaons and pions of rest masses \(M_p=0.94\), \(M_K=0.50\), \(M_\pi=0.14\) in units of \(\mathrm{GeV}/c^2\). The radiator contains air at pressure \(P\), with \(n=1+aP\) and \(a=2.7\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{atm^{-1}}\). Both \(pc\) and \(Mc^2\) are energies. One electronvolt is the energy gained by an electron across a potential difference of one volt. \(1\,\mathrm{GeV}=10^9\,\mathrm{eV}\) and \(1\,\mathrm{MeV}=10^6\,\mathrm{eV}\).

3.1 1.25 points Calculate the threshold air pressure \(P_{\min}\) for each species.

3.2 1.25 points Find the pressure \(P_{1/2}\) for which the kaon ring has half the pion-ring radius. Find both radiation angles. Will a proton ring appear?

Momentum resolution2 points

The momentum distribution is centred on \(10\,\mathrm{GeV}/c\) with half-width at half-maximum \(\Delta p\). The corresponding angular half-width is \(\Delta\theta\). The pressure is \(P_{1/2}\).

4.1 1 point Calculate \(\Delta\theta_K/\Delta p\) and \(\Delta\theta_\pi/\Delta p\).

4.2 1 point Two rings are well separated when \(\theta_\pi-\theta_K>10(\Delta\theta_K+\Delta\theta_\pi)\). Find the limiting momentum half-width.

Radiation in water1.75 points

Cherenkov first noticed the glow of water near a radioactive source. Water has refractive index \(n=1.33\).

5.1 1 point Find the threshold kinetic energy \(T_{\min}\) in water for a particle of mass \(M\).

5.2 0.75 points A helium nucleus (an \(\alpha\) particle) has mass \(3.8\,\mathrm{GeV}/c^2\) and an electron (a \(\beta\) particle) has mass \(0.51\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^2\). Find both thresholds. Which particles produced the observed light if their energies did not exceed a few MeV?

Dispersion and ring colour1.25 points

The continuous spectrum spans the visible band from \(0.4\) to \(0.8\,\mathrm{\mu m}\). Across the band the index decreases linearly by \(2\%\) of \(n-1\).

6.1 0.5 points Pions of fixed momentum \(10.0\,\mathrm{GeV}/c\) travel through air at \(P=6\,\mathrm{atm}\). Find the angular difference \(\delta\theta\) between the ends of the visible band.

6.2.1 0.5 points Now take a momentum half-width \(\Delta p=0.3\,\mathrm{GeV}/c\) about the same mean momentum. Find the angular half-widths due to dispersion and momentum spread.

6.2.2 0.25 points Describe the colour from the inner edge of the ring to the outer edge.
