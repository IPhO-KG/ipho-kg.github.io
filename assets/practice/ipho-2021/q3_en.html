Question T3 [10 pts]. Particles and waves

Parts A and B model electrons confined in a molecule. Parts C and D concern cold atomic gases. Write \(h\) for Planck's constant and \(\hbar=h/(2\pi)\) for the reduced constant.

T3-A [1.4 pts]. Particle in a box

A particle of mass \(m\) is confined by infinite walls at \(x=0,L\) with zero potential between them. Allowed states are standing de Broglie waves with nodes at both walls.

T3-A1 [0.4 pts]. Find the lowest allowed energy in terms of \(m,L,h\).
T3-A2 [0.6 pts]. Index the levels by increasing energy and determine \(E_n\) for \(n=1,2,\ldots\).
T3-A3 [0.4 pts]. Find the wavelength of a photon emitted in a transition from the first excited state to the ground state.

T3-B [2.1 pts]. Spectrum of a molecular dye

Model the ten mobile electrons of the Cy5 backbone as noninteracting particles in a one-dimensional box of width \(L=10.5l\), with mean bond length \(l=140\,\mathrm{pm}\). Each spatial level admits opposite spins. Enforce the Pauli principle and neglect nuclear motion.

T3-B1 [0.8 pts]. For the electronic ground state determine the longest wavelength that Cy5 can absorb. Give a formula in \(l\) and a numerical prediction.
T3-B2 [0.4 pts]. Cy3 has two fewer backbone carbon atoms. Removing them shortens the box by \(2l\) without changing its shape. Determine the direction and size of the absorption shift.
T3-B3 [0.7 pts]. Use dimensions to find the emission rate \(K\) from \(\varepsilon_0,h\), wavelength \(\lambda\) and the transition dipole moment \(d\). The dimensionless factor is \(16\pi^3/3\).
T3-B4 [0.2 pts]. For Cy5 take \(d\simeq2.4el\), where \(e\) is the elementary charge. Estimate the radiative lifetime of its first excited state.

T3-C [1.5 pts]. Bose–Einstein condensation

For an ideal gas of rubidium-87 estimate the onset of condensation by equating the de Broglie wavelength evaluated at the root-mean-square speed to the characteristic spacing of atoms.

T3-C1 [0.4 pts]. For equilibrium temperature \(T\), express the characteristic momentum and wavelength in terms of atomic mass \(m\) and \(T\).
T3-C2 [0.5 pts]. Estimate the atomic spacing \(\ell\) at number density \(n\) and deduce the critical temperature \(T_c\).
T3-C3 [0.6 pts]. At \(T_c=100\,\mathrm{nK}\) calculate \(n_c\) for atoms of mass \(87\,\mathrm u\). Compare with the ideal-gas number density \(n_0\) at \(300\,\mathrm K\) and \(10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}\), giving both \(n_0\) and the ratio.

T3-D [5 pts]. Three-beam optical lattice

Three coherent plane waves of equal intensity and frequency propagate in the \(xy\) plane, separated by \(120^\circ\). Choose \(\mathbf k_1\) along \(x\). All polarizations point along \(z\). Each scalar field is \(E_i=E_0\cos(\mathbf k_i\cdot\mathbf r-\omega t)\) and the atomic potential is \(V=-\alpha\langle(\sum_i E_i)^2\rangle_t\) with \(\alpha>0\).

T3-D1 [1.4 pts]. Express \(V(x,y)\) as a constant plus three cosines of \(\mathbf b_i\cdot\mathbf r\). Identify the vectors \(\mathbf b_i\).
T3-D2 [0.5 pts]. Explain the invariance of the potential under a \(60^\circ\) rotation about the origin.
T3-D3 [1.2 pts]. Derive \(V_X(x)=V(x,0)\) and \(V_Y(y)=V(0,y)\). Identify one representative of each family of maxima and minima of those one-dimensional functions.
T3-D4 [0.8 pts]. Distinguish true sites from saddles and locate all potential minima nearest to the origin. Express the lattice constant in terms of \(\lambda_{\mathrm{las}}\).
T3-D5 [1.1 pts]. A Rydberg electron in rubidium follows a circular orbit with angular momentum \(n\hbar\). Estimate \(n\) when its orbit radius equals \(\lambda_{\mathrm{las}}=380\,\mathrm{nm}\). Give the formula and numerical value.
