Question T1 [10 pts]. Planetary physics

Treat the surface as locally flat in each of the two independent models. Gravity is uniform. For small corrections you may use \((1+u)^s\simeq1+su\).

T1-A [5 pts]. Mid-ocean ridge

Two parallel vertical partitions of width \(w\) seal against the vessel sides. Their lower edges are at depth \(h\) below the outer water surface. Water has density \(\rho_0\). Oil of density \(\rho_{\mathrm{oil}}<\rho_0\) is poured between the partitions until the interface reaches the lower edges. Ignore overflow, mixing and capillarity. Positive \(x\) points right.

T1-A1 [0.8 pts]. Find the magnitude and direction of the horizontal resultant on the right partition.

A ridge of length \(L\) along \(y\) has the same cross-section everywhere. Water \((\rho_0,T_0)\) overlies a freely flexible crust on fluid mantle \((\rho_1,T_1)\). Take the crest as origin and depth \(z\) positive downwards. Crustal thickness increases from zero at \(x=0\) to \(D\) far away, where the seafloor is lower by \(h\). The top and bottom temperatures are \(T_0\) and \(T_1\); far away the profile is linear. Crust at \(T_1\) has density \(\rho_1\). A crust sample has length \(l(T)=l_1[1-k_l(T_1-T)/(T_1-T_0)]\), with \(|k_l|\ll1\).

T1-A2 [0.6 pts]. For an isotropic sample find the density through first order in \(k_l\). Denote the coefficient of \((T_1-T)/(T_1-T_0)\) in \(\rho/\rho_1\) by \(k\).
T1-A3 [1.1 pts]. With the water and mantle incompressible and at rest, determine \(D\) from \(h,\rho_0,\rho_1\) and \(k>0\).
T1-A4 [1.6 pts]. Find the horizontal pressure force on the crust for \(x>0\). Eliminate \(D\) and keep the leading term for small \(k\).
T1-A5 [0.9 pts]. The crust is now insulated from its surroundings. Estimate its thermal relaxation time far from the axis given specific heat \(c\) and conductivity \(\kappa\).

T1-B [5 pts]. Seismic rays

A pulsed line source at the surface origin emits longitudinal waves uniformly in angle into the lower half-plane. The medium is horizontally stratified. Speed at depth \(z\) is \(v_0(1+z/z_0)\), with \(z_0>0\), and rays are circular arcs.

T1-B1 [1.5 pts]. A ray initially makes angle \(\theta_0\) with the downward vertical and travels to the right. Where does it return to the surface? Find \(A,b\) in \(x_1=A\cot(b\theta_0)\) for \(0<\theta_0<\pi/2\).
T1-B2 [1.5 pts]. The source emits energy \(E\) per unit length. If every returning ray is absorbed, find and sketch the received energy per unit surface area \(\varepsilon(x)\). You may retain the general expression from B1.
T1-B3 [2 pts]. Now assume perfect reflection. The source fills the interval \(\theta_0-\delta\theta_0/2\) to \(\theta_0+\delta\theta_0/2\), with \(\delta\theta_0\ll\min(1,\theta_0)\). Estimate how far out the last gap in surface coverage lies.
