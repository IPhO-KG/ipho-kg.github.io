Problem T3 [9 points]. Protostar formation

A spherical cloud of sparse interstellar gas starts from rest and contracts under its own gravity. Its mass is \(m\) and its initial radius \(r_0\). Its surroundings are much more tenuous. Cloud and surroundings initially share a uniform temperature \(T_0\). Treat the gas as ideal, of mean molar mass \(\mu\) and adiabatic index \(\gamma>4/3\). Assume \(Gm\mu/r_0\gg RT_0\), with gas constant \(R\) and gravitational constant \(G\).

T3-i [0.8 points]. Initially the gas is transparent, so it immediately radiates the heat produced and stays in thermal equilibrium with its surroundings. By what factor \(n\) does the pressure increase by the time \(r_1=r_0/2\)? Assume spatially uniform density.

T3-ii [1 point]. Estimate the time \(t_2\) to contract to \(r_2=0.95r_0\), neglecting the change in the gravitational field experienced by a falling gas element.

T3-iii [2.5 points]. Assume pressure remains dynamically negligible. Use Kepler's laws for elliptical orbits to find the time \(t_{r\to0}\) to collapse from \(r_0\) to a radius much smaller than the initial one.

T3-iv [1.7 points]. At radius \(r_3\ll r_0\), the cloud becomes opaque to thermal radiation. How much heat \(Q\) was radiated during the contraction from \(r_0\) to \(r_3\)?

T3-v [1 point]. Below \(r_3\), radiation losses are negligible. Find the temperature \(T\) as a function of radius.

T3-vi [2 points]. Pressure eventually halts the collapse at \(r_4\ll r_3\). Radiation remains negligible and fusion has not begun. The pressure in the protostar is now nonuniform. Estimate \(r_4\) and the corresponding temperature \(T_4\) up to numerical factors of order unity.
