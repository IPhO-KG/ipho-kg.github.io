Question T3 [10 points]. Black widow pulsar

In a compact binary, a neutron star can gain mass from its companion. Consider how mass transfer changes the orbit, how the resulting accretion disc heats up, and how a single star responds to radial perturbations.

T3-A [5 points]. The binary orbit

Two point masses \(M_1\) and \(M_2\) follow circular orbits at a separation \(a\). Choose axes rotating with the stars about their common centre of mass, with the stars on the \(x\) axis and \(M_1\) at negative \(x\). In the orbital plane the effective potential per unit mass includes gravity and the centrifugal term.

T3-A1 [1 point]. Find \(\varphi(x,y)\) in terms of \(M_1,M_2,G,a\).

T3-A2 [0.7 points]. Sketch \(\varphi(x,0)\) for \(M_1>M_2\).

T3-A3 [0.5 points]. In this part only, let \(M_2=M_1/3\). A tenuous envelope of negligible mass surrounds \(M_2\). As it expands, gas begins to escape towards \(M_1\) through a point \(x_0\) on the axis. Calculate \(x_0/a\) to two significant figures.

Let \(P\) be the period. Mass passes from \(M_2\) to \(M_1\) slowly with \(\dot M_1=\beta\). The system conserves total mass and orbital angular momentum. The slowly evolving orbit can be treated as circular at each instant.

T3-A4 [0.6 points]. Express \(\dot a\) and \(\dot P\) in terms of \(\beta,M_1,M_2,G,a\).

In a thin steady disc about \(M_1\), gas spirals inward through nearly circular orbits. Mass \(\beta\) crosses each radius per unit time. Locally dissipated energy is emitted as blackbody radiation from both faces. Neglect the gas heat capacity and the gravity of \(M_2\) in the disc.

T3-A5 [1 point]. Find the disc temperature at radius \(r\) in terms of \(r,M_1,G,\sigma,\beta\).

For PSR J2215+5135 use \(M_1=2.27M_\odot,\ M_2=0.33M_\odot,\ M_\odot=1.98\times10^{30}\,\mathrm{kg},\ P=4.14\,\mathrm h,\ \beta=9\times10^{-10}M_\odot/\mathrm{yr}\). Take \(G=6.67\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}s^{-2}}\) and \(\sigma=5.67\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}K^{-4}}\).

T3-A6 [0.5 points]. Calculate \(T\) in kelvins at \(r=a/10\).

T3-A7 [0.7 points]. Suppose the first star suddenly ejects mass, leaving a remnant \(M_1^\prime\) moving at \(v^\prime\) relative to \(M_2\). Find the limiting relative speed for a bound binary. If the ejection is isotropic, find the minimum surviving mass.

T3-B [5 points]. Stellar stability

Consider an isolated spherical star with equation of state \(p=K\rho^\gamma\), with constant \(K,\gamma\). At radius \(r\) the pressure and density are \(p(r),\rho(r)\), taking central values \(p_c,\rho_c\). Radially outward is positive.

T3-B1 [0.2 points]. Find the gravitational acceleration near the centre using \(r,G,\rho_c\).

T3-B2 [0.6 points]. Derive the equilibrium density equation in the form \(\frac{d}{dr}[h_1(\rho,r)\frac{d\rho}{dr}]+h_2(r)\rho=0\) and identify \(h_1,h_2\).

T3-B3 [0.4 points]. Find the exponents in \(r_0=G^\ell p_c^m\rho_c^n\) that give dimensions of length.

T3-B4 [0.3 points]. With \(x=r/r_0\) and \(u=\rho/\rho_c\), express the equation as \(\frac{d}{dx}[A_1(u,x)\frac{du}{dx}]+A_2(x)u=0\) and find both coefficients.

T3-B5 [0.6 points]. For \(\gamma=2\) write \(u=f(x)/x\) and determine \(f(x)\).

T3-B6 [0.8 points]. Use the graph of \(du/dx\) close to \(x=0\) to estimate \(\gamma\) to three significant figures. A ruler may be used to measure the tangent.

To test stability, perturb shells near the centre. A shell formerly at \(r\) moves to \(\widetilde r=r[1+\epsilon(t)]\), where \(|\epsilon|\ll1\). Write \(\widetilde g,\widetilde p,\widetilde\rho\) for its new quantities and keep the enclosed mass fixed.

T3-B7 [0.9 points]. Express \(\widetilde\rho\) and \(\widetilde g\) in terms of \(\rho,g,\epsilon\) to first order.

T3-B8 [0.6 points]. Find \(d^2\widetilde r/dt^2\) using \(\widetilde g,\widetilde\rho,K,\gamma\) and the density gradient \(\partial\widetilde\rho/\partial\widetilde r\) at fixed time.

T3-B9 [0.6 points]. Derive the linear evolution equation for \(\epsilon(t)\). Determine the lower stability threshold in \(\gamma\) and the angular frequency of oscillation.
