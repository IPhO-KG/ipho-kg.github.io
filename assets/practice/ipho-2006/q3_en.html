Problem T3 [10 points]. Five order-of-magnitude estimates

The five parts are independent. Order-of-magnitude estimates are sufficient throughout.

Digital camera 2.5 pts

A square CCD chip of side \(L=35\,\mathrm{mm}\) has \(N_p=5\) megapixels (\(1\) Mpix \(=10^6\) pixels). The lens focal length is \(f=38\,\mathrm{mm}\). Its f-number \(F=f/D\) uses the circular aperture diameter \(D\). The available stops are \(2,\ 2.8,\ 4,\ 5.6,\ 8,\ 11,\ 16,\ 22\).

3.1 [1 pts]. What is the finest spatial resolution on the chip allowed by the lens? Give it in terms of wavelength \(\lambda\) and \(F\), and calculate it at \(\lambda=500\,\mathrm{nm}\).
3.2 [0.5 pts]. How many megapixels would the chip need to match that optimal resolution?
3.3 [0.5 pts]. Another camera has the same chip size and focal length but \(N_0=16\) Mpix. Which f-number gives the smallest available aperture for which image quality is still not limited by the optics?
3.4 [0.5 pts]. The eye resolves about two arcminutes. From what minimum viewing distance \(z\) do the dots of a print at \(300\) dots per inch cease to be individually visible? One inch is \(25.4\,\mathrm{mm}\) and one arcminute is \(2.91\times10^{-4}\) rad.

Heating an egg 2 pts

An egg at refrigerator temperature \(T_0=4^\circ\mathrm C\) is put into water kept boiling at \(T_1\). Use radius \(R=2.5\,\mathrm{cm}\), density \(\mu=10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}\) and specific heat \(C=4.2\,\mathrm{J/(g\,K)}\). The albumen coagulates at \(T_c=65^\circ\mathrm C\). The thermal conductivity \(\kappa=0.64\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}\) is the same before and after coagulation. Estimate the heat flux in watts per square metre from Fourier’s law \(J\simeq\kappa\Delta T/\Delta r\) over a characteristic length \(\Delta r\).

3.5 [0.5 pts]. Estimate the energy \(U\) required to bring the whole egg to coagulation temperature.
3.6 [0.5 pts]. Estimate the heat flux \(J\) into the egg.
3.7 [0.5 pts]. What thermal power \(P\) is transferred to the egg?
3.8 [0.5 pts]. How long, \(\tau\), does the model predict for hard-boiling the egg?

Lightning 2 pts

Charge separation in a cloud induces charge on the ground. In the model the cloud base is positive, its top is negative and the ground below is negative. A lightning discharge occurs at the breakdown field. Use cloud-base height \(h=1\,\mathrm{km}\), humid-air breakdown field \(E_0=300\,\mathrm{kV/m}\), \(32\times10^6\) discharges worldwide per year and a population of \(6.5\times10^9\).

3.9 [0.5 pts]. How much charge \(Q\) is transferred in one discharge?
3.10 [0.5 pts]. What current \(I\) flows on average between cloud and ground during the pulse?
3.11 [1 pts]. Imagine sharing the energy of all lightning over one year equally among the population. How long could one person’s share run a \(100\,\mathrm W\) lamp continuously?

Capillary vessels 1.5 pts

Model blood as an incompressible viscous fluid of water-like density, with dynamic viscosity \(\eta=4.5\,\mathrm{g/(m\,s)}\). For a straight cylindrical vessel of radius \(r\) and length \(L\), Poiseuille’s law is \(\Delta p=R_hD\), with resistance \(R_h=8\eta L/(\pi r^4)\) and volume flow \(D=Su\), where \(u\) is the mean speed over area \(S\). Resting systemic circulation carries a total flow of about \(100\,\mathrm{cm^3/s}\). Treat all capillaries as parallel pipes, each with \(r=4\,\mu\mathrm m\), \(L=1\,\mathrm{mm}\) and pressure drop \(\Delta p=1\,\mathrm{kPa}\).

3.12 [1 pts]. Estimate the total number \(N\) of capillaries.
3.13 [0.5 pts]. At what speed \(u\) does the blood flow in one capillary?

Air temperature at the top of a skyscraper 2 pts

At the foot of a \(1000\,\mathrm m\) skyscraper the outside air is at \(T_{\rm bot}=30^\circ\mathrm C\). A thin air parcel rises slowly into lower pressure and expands adiabatically. Its temperature equals that of the local atmosphere as it rises. Model the air as ideal nitrogen with \(\gamma=7/5\). Use \(k=1.38\times10^{-23}\,\mathrm{J/K}\), molecular mass \(m=4.65\times10^{-26}\,\mathrm{kg}\) and \(g=9.80\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\).

3.14 [0.5 pts]. Relate the fractional temperature change \(dT/T\) to the fractional pressure change \(dp/p\).
3.15 [0.5 pts]. Express the pressure change \(dp\) in terms of the height increment \(dz\).
3.16 [1 pts]. Estimate the outside air temperature at the top of the building.
