Question T2 [11 pts]. Van der Waals equation of state

Consider one mole of water throughout. Write \(V\) for molar volume, \(P\) for pressure and \(T\) for temperature. You may use \(R=8.31\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}\) and \(N_A=6.02\cdot10^{23}\,\mathrm{mol^{-1}}\).

T2-A [2 pts]. Equation of state of a non-ideal gas

A first correction to the ideal gas accounts for molecular size. The available volume is reduced by \(b\), giving \(P(V-b)=RT\).

T2-A1 [0.3 pts]. Estimate \(b\) in terms of molecular diameter \(d\).

Attraction between molecules introduces a second parameter \(a\). The equation of state becomes

\[\left(P+\frac a{V^2}\right)(V-b)=RT.\]

Below the critical temperature \(T_c\) the calculated isotherm develops a loop. In equilibrium the liquid and vapour coexist along a horizontal segment \(AB\) at pressure \(P_{LG}\), from \(V_L\) to \(V_G\). Maxwell's construction gives equal areas I and II. At \(T=T_c\) the segment contracts to a point at pressure \(P_c\).

T2-A2 [1.3 pts]. Express \(a\) and \(b\) using \(T_c\) and \(P_c\).
T2-A3 [0.2 pts]. Water has \(T_c=647\,\mathrm K\) and \(P_c=2.2\cdot10^7\,\mathrm{Pa}\). Evaluate \(a_w\) and \(b_w\).
T2-A4 [0.2 pts]. Estimate the diameter \(d_w\) of a water molecule.

T2-C [3 pts]. Liquid–vapour equilibrium

Maxwell's construction in the approximations of Part B gives the saturation pressure over a flat surface as

\[\ln p_{LG}=A+\frac B T,\qquad A=\ln\left(\frac a{b^2}\right)-1,\qquad B=-\frac a{bR}.\]

The liquid does not wet the capillary and has a contact angle of \(180^\circ\). The liquid surface inside the tube is depressed below the flat surface and has a convex meniscus.

T2-C1 [1.3 pts]. Find the small change \(\Delta p_T\) in saturated vapour pressure above the curved liquid. Use vapour and liquid densities \(\rho_s,\rho_L\), surface tension \(\sigma\) and radius of curvature \(r\).
