Question T3 [10 pts]. Why are stars so large?

Most stars shine by fusing hydrogen into helium in their cores. Estimate the temperature and pressure this requires and how they constrain a star's radius and mass. Give numerical results to two significant figures.

Use the following constants and solar parameters.

Gravitational constant\(G=6.7\cdot10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}\)
Boltzmann constant\(k=1.4\cdot10^{-23}\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}\)
Planck constant\(h=6.6\cdot10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}\)
Proton mass\(m_p=1.7\cdot10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}\)
Electron mass\(m_e=9.1\cdot10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}\)
Elementary charge\(q=1.6\cdot10^{-19}\,\mathrm C\)
Vacuum permittivity\(\varepsilon_0=8.9\cdot10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2\,N^{-1}\,m^{-2}}\)
Solar radius\(R_{\odot}=7.0\cdot10^8\,\mathrm m\)
Solar mass\(M_{\odot}=2.0\cdot10^{30}\,\mathrm{kg}\)

1 1.5 pts Classical temperature estimate

Model the star as an ideal gas of pure ionised hydrogen. Two point protons approach each other head-on, each at the proton rms speed \(v_{\rm rms}\). They must overcome Coulomb repulsion and reach a separation \(d_c=10^{-15}\,\mathrm m\) before the attractive strong interaction dominates.

1a 1.5 pts At what temperature \(T_c\) is the distance of closest approach equal to \(d_c\)?

2 2 pts Hydrostatic cross-check

In equilibrium the pressure difference across a thin gas layer supports its weight. At radius \(r\) the enclosed mass is \(M_r\) and the local gas density is \(\rho_r\).

\[\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta r}=-\frac{GM_r\rho_r}{r^2}.\]

For an order-of-magnitude estimate take \(\Delta P=P_{\rm surf}-P_c\simeq-P_c\), \(r\simeq\Delta r\simeq R\), \(M_r\simeq M\) and \(\rho_r\simeq\rho_c\). Here \(M,R\) are the stellar mass and radius, and the subscript \(c\) denotes the centre. Use the ideal-gas equation of state.

2a 0.5 pts Express \(T_c\) in terms of \(M\), \(R\) and physical constants.
2b 0.5 pts Write \(M/R\) using \(T_c\) and physical constants.
2c 0.5 pts Insert the temperature from 1a and calculate \(M/R\).
2d 0.5 pts Calculate \(M_{\odot}/R_{\odot}\) and compare it with 2c.

3 2 pts Quantum temperature estimate

Include the wave character of protons. In this model fusion becomes possible when their wave packets overlap at closest approach. Set \(d_c=\lambda_p/\sqrt2\), where \(\lambda_p\) is the de Broglie wavelength at speed \(v_{\rm rms}\).

3a 1 pts Obtain a new estimate for \(T_c\) in terms of physical constants only.
3b 0.5 pts Calculate the new temperature.
3c 0.5 pts Insert 3b into 2b, calculate \(M/R\) and compare it with the solar ratio.

4 0.5 pts Stellar mass-to-radius ratio

For hydrogen-burning main-sequence stars the model roughly reproduces their characteristic mass-to-radius ratio.

4a 0.5 pts Show that the predicted \(M/R\) for hydrogen fusion contains only physical constants. Write this expression.

5 3.5 pts The smallest hydrogen-burning star

At fixed \(M/R\), shrinking the radius increases the density. The electron gas becomes degenerate when the electron wavelength is comparable to the particle spacing. For this estimate take a uniform density, use \(T_c\) throughout and neglect the electron mass when computing the mass of the star.

5a 0.5 pts Find the mean electron number density \(n_e\).
5b 0.5 pts Find the characteristic electron spacing \(d_e\).
5c 1.5 pts Require \(d_e\ge\lambda_e/\sqrt2\) for nondegenerate electrons. Derive the minimum stellar radius.
5d 0.5 pts Calculate the radius in metres and in solar radii.
5e 0.5 pts Find the corresponding mass in kilograms and in solar masses.

6 0.5 pts Helium fusion

A star that has exhausted the hydrogen in its core may fuse helium into heavier nuclei. A helium nucleus has charge \(2q\) and mass \(m_{\rm He}\simeq4m_p\).

6a 0.5 pts State the overlap condition for helium nuclei. Find their rms speed and the helium-fusion temperature in this model.
