Question T1 [10 pts]. Evolution of the Earth–Moon system

Timing a laser pulse reflected by a lunar retroreflector gives the Earth–Moon distance. Repeated measurements show that the Moon is receding. Relate this change to tides and to the slowing of the Earth's rotation. Report numerical results to two significant figures.

1 0.7 pts Conservation of angular momentum

Include the Earth's spin and the circular orbit of a point-like Moon around the Earth's centre. The spin and orbital axes are parallel. All moments and torques are taken about the Earth's axis. Neglect the Sun and the Earth's motion about the barycentre.

1a 0.2 pts Express today's total angular momentum \(L_1\) using the Earth's inertia \(I_E\), the Moon's orbital inertia \(I_{M1}\) and the respective angular speeds \(\omega_{E1},\omega_{M1}\).
1b 0.2 pts In the final state the Earth and Moon share angular speed \(\omega_2\) and the bulges align with the line of centres. Express \(L_2\) using \(I_E\), the final orbital inertia \(I_{M2}\) and \(\omega_2\).
1c 0.3 pts Write the conservation law neglecting the Earth's spin contribution only in the final state.

2 2.9 pts The final lunar orbit

Treat the orbit as circular throughout and neglect the final spin contribution as in 1c. Denote the masses by \(M_E,M_M\) and the final centre-to-centre distance by \(D_2\).

2a 0.2 pts Relate \(D_2,\omega_2,G\) and \(M_E\).
2b 0.5 pts Find \(D_2\) in terms of \(L_1,M_E,M_M,G\).
2c 0.5 pts Express \(\omega_2\) in terms of the same parameters.
2d 0.5 pts A core of radius \(r_i\) has density \(\rho_i\). The shell from \(r_i\) to \(r_o\) has density \(\rho_o\). Find \(I_E\).
2e 0.2 pts Evaluate \(I_E\) for \(\rho_i=1.3\cdot10^4\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}\), \(r_i=3.5\cdot10^6\,\mathrm m\), \(\rho_o=4.0\cdot10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}\) and \(r_o=6.4\cdot10^6\,\mathrm m\).

Use \(M_E=6.0\cdot10^{24}\,\mathrm{kg}\), \(M_M=7.3\cdot10^{22}\,\mathrm{kg}\), \(D_1=3.8\cdot10^8\,\mathrm m\), \(\omega_{E1}=7.3\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\), \(\omega_{M1}=2.7\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\) and \(G=6.7\cdot10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}s^{-2}}\) below.

2f 0.2 pts Evaluate \(L_1\).
2g 0.3 pts Calculate \(D_2\) in metres and in units of \(D_1\).
2h 0.3 pts Calculate \(\omega_2\) and the final day length in present-day units.
2i 0.2 pts Find the final ratio of the Earth's spin angular momentum to the Moon's orbital angular momentum.

3 5.1 pts Rate of lunar recession

Represent the two tidal bulges by point masses \(m\) at opposite points on the Earth's surface. Their axis leads the Earth–Moon direction by angle \(\theta\). Consider the present tidal torque.

3a 0.4 pts Find the magnitude \(F_c\) of the force on the Moon due to the nearer mass \(m\).
3b 0.4 pts Find the magnitude \(F_f\) due to the farther mass.
3c 0.4 pts Find the magnitude \(\tau_c\) of the torque due to the near bulge.
3d 0.4 pts Find the magnitude \(\tau_f\) of the torque due to the far bulge.
3e 1 pts Obtain the net torque magnitude to the lowest nonzero order in \(r_o/D_1\ll1\). You may use \((1+x)^a\simeq1+ax\).
3f 0.5 pts Evaluate \(\tau\) for \(\theta=3^\circ\) and \(m=3.6\cdot10^{16}\,\mathrm{kg}\).
3g 1 pts Express lunar orbital angular momentum via \(M_M,M_E,D_1,G\) and find the present annual increase in distance.
3h 1 pts Find the annual changes in the Earth's angular speed and day length.

4 1.3 pts Energy dissipation

4a 0.4 pts Express the total mechanical energy using \(I_E,\omega_{E1},M_M,M_E,D_1,G\).
4b 0.4 pts Express \(\Delta E\) through the changes in \(D_1,\omega_{E1}\) and evaluate it for one year using 3g and 3h.

Model the oceans as a global layer of depth \(h=0.5\,\mathrm m\). Twice a day the tide raises it by a mean height of \(0.5\,\mathrm m\). During descent one tenth of the gained potential energy becomes heat. Use \(\rho_w=10^3\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}\) and \(g=9.8\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\).

4c 0.2 pts What mass is lifted?
