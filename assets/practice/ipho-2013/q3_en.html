Question T3 [9 points]. The Greenlandic ice sheet

Model Greenland as a rectangular island of width \(2L\) and length \(5L\), with bedrock at sea level. Incompressible ice of density \(\rho_i=917\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}\) covers the entire island. The height \(H(x)\) is greatest at the central ice divide \(x=0\) and falls to zero at the coasts \(x=\pm L\). The profile is independent of \(y\) for \(0<y<5L\) and ends abruptly at the two ends. You may use \(\int_0^1\sqrt{1-u}\,du=2/3\) and \((1+u)^a\simeq1+au\) when \(\lvert au\rvert\ll1\).

Ice-sheet profile2.5 points

3.1 0.3 points On short time scales the profile is fixed and the ice is in hydrostatic equilibrium. Find \(p(x,z)\) at height \(z\) above the bed, ignoring atmospheric pressure.

A vertical slab between \(x\) and \(x+\Delta x\) has base area \(\Delta x\Delta y\). The net horizontal pressure force on its sides is balanced by basal friction of magnitude \(S_b\Delta x\Delta y\), where \(S_b=100\,\mathrm{kPa}\). Consider the half-sheet \(x>0\).

3.2a 0.9 points In the limit \(\Delta x\to0\) derive \(S_b=kH\,dH/dx\) and find \(k\).

3.2b 0.8 points Find \(H(x)\) in terms of \(\rho_i,g,L,S_b,x\) and show how the maximum \(H_m\) depends on the half-width \(L\).

3.2c 0.5 points Find the exponent \(\gamma\) relating ice volume to island area, \(V_i\propto A^\gamma\).

Ice flow2.1 points

Over long times the ice flows as an incompressible viscous fluid, away from the divide in the \(xz\) plane. In steady state it accumulates in the interior at a constant rate \(c\) (ice thickness per year) and is lost only by melting near the coasts. Take \(v_x=v_x(x)\) and \(v_z=v_z(z)\). In the central region \(\lvert x\rvert\ll L\), approximate \(H(x)\simeq H_m\).

3.3 0.6 points Use mass conservation to express \(v_x(x)\) in terms of \(c,x,H_m\).

3.4 0.6 points Use incompressibility, \(dv_x/dx+dv_z/dz=0\), to obtain \(v_z(z)\).

3.5 0.9 points An ice particle starts at \((x_i,H_m)\) on the surface. Find its trajectory \(z(x)\) within the central region.

Ice age and climate records2 points

3.6 1 point At the divide \(x=0\), find the age \(\tau(z)\) of ice at height \(z\) above bedrock.

Let \(R\) be the abundance ratio of stable oxygen isotopes \(^{18}\mathrm O/^{16}\mathrm O\). The deviation \(\delta^{18}\mathrm O=1000(R_{\mathrm{ice}}-R_{\mathrm{ref}})/R_{\mathrm{ref}}\) is expressed in per mille. The reference is equatorial ocean water. Assume that the relation between snow isotope composition and annual mean temperature in Fig. 3.2(a) also applied in the past.

The divide core reaches bedrock at \(H_m=3060\,\mathrm m\). The beginning of the ice age 120,000 years ago is at 3040 m depth. The abrupt jump at 1492 m marks the present interglacial’s beginning 11,700 years ago. Each period has its own constant accumulation rate. Keep \(H_m\) fixed throughout.

3.7a 0.8 points Determine the ice-age accumulation rate \(c_{\mathrm{ia}}\) and the interglacial rate \(c_{\mathrm{ig}}\).

3.7b 0.2 points Use both panels of Fig. 3.2 to estimate the temperature change at the start of the interglacial.

Sea level2.4 points

3.8 0.6 points Estimate the uniform global sea-level rise if the Greenland ice sheet melts completely. The ice area is \(A_G=1.71\times10^{12}\,\mathrm{m^2}\), the constant ocean area is \(A_o=3.61\times10^{14}\,\mathrm{m^2}\) and \(S_b=100\,\mathrm{kPa}\).

The ice also attracts nearby seawater. Before melting, represent it as a point mass on the surface of a spherically symmetric Earth. Let an ocean cover the entire Earth, whose area is \(5.10\times10^{14}\,\mathrm{m^2}\). Copenhagen is 3500 km from the ice mass along the surface. Neglect rotation.

3.9 1.8 points Find the sea-level difference \(h_{\mathrm{CPH}}-h_{\mathrm{OPP}}\) between Copenhagen and the point diametrically opposite Greenland.
