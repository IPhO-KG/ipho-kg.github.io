Question T1 [10 pts]. Zero-length springs and slinky coils

A uniform spring of mass \(M\) has length \(L_0\) when its turns touch. Once the turns separate, its tension is proportional to the entire length, \(F=kL\). Before separation, the length stays at \(L_0\). Springs with this property are used in gravimetry, and some slinky toys behave this way. Define \(\alpha=kL_0/(Mg)<1\).

T1-A [3 pts]. Static equilibrium

T1-A1 [0.5 pts]. A tensile force \(F\) acts on the spring in weightlessness. Find the new length \(\Delta y\) of a segment initially of length \(\Delta\ell\) in terms of \(F,\Delta\ell\) and the spring parameters.
T1-A2 [0.5 pts]. Find the work \(\Delta W\) needed to lengthen this segment from \(\Delta\ell\) to \(\Delta y\).

Label each turn by its distance \(\ell\) from the lower end in the original, closed spring. Each material label \(0\le\ell\le L_0\) stays with its turn.

T1-A3 [2 pts]. Suspend the upper end and let the spring settle. Find its equilibrium length \(H\) in terms of \(L_0\) and \(\alpha\).

T1-B [5.5 pts]. Collapse dynamics

Release the top from rest. A compact moving bundle grows downwards by collecting successive turns. Each lower turn stays at its original position until it joins the bundle. The bottom starts to fall only when the whole spring has contracted to \(L_0\). It then falls intact without tumbling. Ignore air drag and retain the finite closed length.

T1-B1 [2.5 pts]. Express the collapse time \(t_c\) in terms of \(L_0,g,\alpha\). Evaluate it for \(k=1.02\,\mathrm{N/m}\), \(L_0=0.055\,\mathrm m\), \(M=0.201\,\mathrm{kg}\) and \(g=9.80\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\).
T1-B2 [2.5 pts]. Let \(\ell\) mark the compression front, with stationary mass \(M\ell/L_0\) remaining below it. Derive \(v_{\rm I}(\ell)=\sqrt{A\ell+B}\) for the compact bundle and find \(A,B\) in terms of \(L_0,g,\alpha\).
T1-B3 [0.5 pts]. Find the minimum speed of the moving material after release and before ground impact in terms of \(L_0,\alpha,A,B\).

T1-C [1.5 pts]. Energy

T1-C1 [1.5 pts]. How much mechanical energy \(Q\) is dissipated between release and the instant before impact? Express it in terms of \(L_0,M,g,\alpha\).
