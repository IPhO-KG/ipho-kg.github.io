Question T3 [10 pts]. Thermoacoustic Engine

A thermoacoustic engine converts heat flow into acoustic energy. The working fluid here is gas in a closed tube. We seek the work that amplifies the sound wave inside the tube.

T3-A [3.7 pts]. Standing wave in a closed tube

An insulated tube of length \(L\) and cross section \(S\) has rigid ends at \(x=0\) and \(x=L\). The ideal gas has equilibrium temperature \(T_0\), pressure \(p_0\) and density \(\rho_0\). Neglect viscosity. Motion is longitudinal and uniform over each cross section. A parcel whose equilibrium coordinate is \(x\) has displacement

\[u(x,t)=a\sin(kx)\cos(\omega t),\qquad k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda},\qquad 0<a\ll L.\]
T3-A1 [0.3 pts]. What is the longest allowed wavelength? Use this mode and small departures from equilibrium throughout.
T3-A2 [0.5 pts]. A parcel initially between \(x\) and \(x+\Delta x\) has volume \(V_0=S\Delta x\), where \(\Delta x\ll L\). Find \(V_1(x)\) in \(V=V_0+V_1(x)\cos(\omega t)\).
T3-A3 [0.7 pts]. Write \(p=p_0-p_1(x)\cos(\omega t)\). Use the equation of motion of the parcel to find \(p_1(x)\) to first order in amplitude.
T3-A4 [0.3 pts]. Treat the oscillations as adiabatic, \(pV^\gamma=\mathrm{const}\). Express the sound speed \(c=\omega/k\) in terms of the gas properties.
T3-A5 [0.7 pts]. Write the temperature as \(T=T_0-T_1(x)\cos(\omega t)\). Find \(T_1(x)\) in terms of \(T_0\), \(\gamma\), \(a\), \(k\) and \(x\).
T3-A6 [1.2 pts]. For this part allow weak heat exchange with the wall and assume the standing wave is essentially unaltered. Decide how each wall temperature changes over time. A and C are the ends and B is at the centre. Neglect viscous heating.

T3-B [6.3 pts]. Thermal amplification of sound

Thin plates parallel to the tube axis fill the cross section over a length \(\ell\ll L\) centred at \(x_0=L/4\), leaving gaps for gas flow. The left end at \(x_H=x_0-\ell/2\) is held at \(T_H=T_0+\tau/2\) and the right end at \(x_C=x_0+\ell/2\) at \(T_C=T_0-\tau/2\). The plate temperature falls linearly across the stack.

\[T_{\mathrm{plate}}(x)=T_0-\frac{\tau}{\ell}(x-x_0).\]

Use the fundamental mode and \(\tau\ll T_0\). Pressure and displacement amplitudes are nearly uniform along the short stack and close to those of the unperturbed wave. Neglect end effects. For the heat budget retain convection and local gas–plate exchange. Direct axial conduction is negligible.

T3-B1 [0.4 pts]. A parcel oscillates about \(x_0\). Write the temperature of the adjacent plate section as \(T_{\mathrm{env}}=T_0-T_{\mathrm{st}}\cos(\omega t)\). Find \(T_{\mathrm{st}}\).
T3-B2 [1 pts]. Above what critical temperature difference \(\tau_{\mathrm{cr}}\) does the gas carry heat from the hot to the cold reservoir? Use \(T_0\), \(\gamma\), \(k\) and \(\ell\).
T3-B3 [0.8 pts]. Express the heat input rate \(\dot Q\) to first order in \(\dot p\) and \(\dot V\). Use \(p_0,V_0,\gamma\) and molar heat capacity \(c_V=R/(\gamma-1)\).

Exchange with the plates shifts the phases. Assume \(\dot Q=-\beta V_0(T_{\mathrm{st}}-T_1)\cos(\omega t)\) with \(\beta>0\). The extra change in gas temperature is small compared with either temperature amplitude. Write the pressure and volume as

\[p=p_0+p_a\sin(\omega t)-p_b\cos(\omega t),\]
\[V=V_0+V_a\sin(\omega t)+V_b\cos(\omega t).\]
T3-B4 [1.9 pts]. For given \(p_a,p_b\) find \(V_a,V_b\) in terms of the wave and heat-transfer parameters and the differences \(\tau,\tau_{\mathrm{cr}}\).
T3-B5 [0.8 pts]. Find the work \(w\) produced per unit gas volume in one cycle and the total \(W_{\mathrm{tot}}\) in the stack. Express \(W_{\mathrm{tot}}\) using \(\gamma,\tau,\tau_{\mathrm{cr}},\beta,\omega,a,k,S\).
T3-B6 [0.8 pts]. How much heat \(Q_{\mathrm{tot}}\) crosses the plane \(x=x_0\) from left to right per cycle? You may use the fact that the convective heat current is proportional to the heat carried and the velocity \(\dot u\).
T3-B7 [0.6 pts]. Find the ratio of acoustic work to the heat drawn from the hot reservoir. Express it in terms of \(\tau\), \(\tau_{\mathrm{cr}}\) and the Carnot efficiency \(\eta_c=1-T_C/T_H\).
