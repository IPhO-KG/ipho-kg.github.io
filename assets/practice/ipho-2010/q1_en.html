Problem T1 [10 pts]. An image charge in a conducting sphere

A point charge \(q\) is a distance \(d>R\) from the centre of a grounded conducting sphere of radius \(R\). Surface charges are induced on the sphere. On and outside its surface, their potential is exactly reproduced by one fictitious charge \(q'\) inside. You may use this property without proof.

1 2.7 pts The image charge

By symmetry the image lies on the line joining the centre to \(q\). Denote its distance from the centre by \(d'\).

1(a) 0.3 pts What is the electric potential on the sphere?

1(b) 1.9 pts Determine \(q'\) and \(d'\) in terms of \(q,d,R\).

1(c) 0.5 pts Find the magnitude of the force on \(q\) and determine whether it is attractive or repulsive.

2 1.5 pts Electrostatic shielding

Point \(A\) lies on the same axis beyond the far side of the sphere. Its distance from the real charge is \(r\).

2(a) 0.6 pts Find the electric field vector at \(A\).

2(b) 0.6 pts Find the field for \(r\gg d\), keeping the first correction with \((1+a)^{-2}\simeq1-2a\) for \(\lvert a\rvert\ll1\).

2(c) 0.3 pts In what limit of \(d\) does the field at \(A\) vanish?

3 2.6 pts Small oscillations of a charged pendulum

A charge \(q\) of mass \(m\) hangs from a thread of length \(L\). Its point of suspension on a wall is a distance \(l\) from the centre of the grounded sphere. Measure \(\alpha\) from the line pointing from the support towards the centre. Ignore gravity and any electrostatic influence of the wall.

3(a) 0.8 pts Find the electric force magnitude for a given \(\alpha\) and show its direction in a sketch.

3(b) 0.8 pts Express the component perpendicular to the thread in terms of \(l,L,R,q,\alpha\).

3(c) 1 pt Find the frequency of small oscillations.

4 3.2 pts Electrostatic energy

Return to the stationary charge of part 1. Express all energies in terms of \(q,R,d\). The induced charges interact both with the external charge and with one another.

4(a) 1 pt Find the interaction energy of \(q\) with the induced charges.

4(b) 1.2 pts Determine the mutual electrostatic energy of the induced charges.

4(c) 1 pt Find the total electrostatic interaction energy of the system.

You may use the integral below, or express the electrostatic energy as the sum of the interactions of all unordered pairs of charges.

\[\int_d^\infty\frac{x\,dx}{(x^2-R^2)^2}=\frac{1}{2(d^2-R^2)} .\]
\[U=\frac12\sum_i\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{q_iq_j}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\lvert\mathbf r_i-\mathbf r_j\rvert}.\]
