Problem T1 [10 points]. Two problems in mechanics

T1-A [3.5 pts]. The hidden disk

A cylinder of wood has radius \(r_1\), thickness \(h_1\) and density \(\rho_1\). A cylindrical region has been replaced by a metal disk of radius \(r_2\), thickness \(h_2\) and density \(\rho_2>\rho_1\). The disk is midway between the end faces. Its axis \(B\) is parallel to the cylinder axis \(S\), at distance \(d\). The quantities \(r_1,h_1,\rho_1,\rho_2\) and the total mass \(M\) are known. Determine the buried disk geometry by external measurements.

Let \(b\) be the distance from \(S\) to the combined centre of mass \(C\). Start with the cylinder resting on its curved side in stable equilibrium. Slowly tilt the support through \(\Theta\). Static friction allows rolling without sliding. Measure the angle \(\phi\) shown in the second equilibrium configuration.

T1-A1 [0.8 pts]. Express \(b\) in terms of \(\phi,\Theta\) and the known parameters.

Now take \(b\) as known. Suspend the cylinder on a rigid axle through \(S\). A small angular displacement \(\varphi\) from equilibrium gives oscillations of measured period \(T\).

T1-A2 [0.5 pts]. Find the small-oscillation equation and the moment of inertia \(I_S\) about \(S\) from \(T,b\) and the known quantities.
T1-A3 [0.4 pts]. Find the offset \(d\) in terms of \(b\) and the known parameters. At this stage \(r_2,h_2\) may also appear.
T1-A4 [0.7 pts]. Derive an independent expression for \(I_S\) using \(b\) and the cylinder and insert parameters. You may include \(r_2,h_2\).
T1-A5 [1.1 pts]. Determine \(r_2\) and \(h_2\) from \(b,T\) and the known quantities. Your result for \(h_2\) may use \(r_2\).

T1-B [6.5 pts]. A rotating space station

Alice and Bob live on the inside rim of a wheel-shaped space station of radius \(R\). Rotation provides artificial gravity. Neglect the mass attraction of the station and approximate the floor as flat locally.

T1-B1 [0.5 pts]. Find the angular speed \(\omega_{\rm ss}\) that reproduces the terrestrial surface gravity \(g_E\) at the rim.

Bob claims they are on Earth. Alice puts a mass \(m\) on a vertical spring of stiffness \(k\) and constrains its motion to the vertical direction.

T1-B2 [0.2 pts]. What angular frequency \(\omega_E\) would a terrestrial observer find in uniform gravity?
T1-B3 [0.6 pts]. Find the angular frequency \(\omega\) measured on the station.
T1-B4 [0.8 pts]. Bob points out the height dependence of terrestrial gravity. Expand the gravitational acceleration at height \(h\ll R_E\) to first order and find the resulting oscillator frequency \(\widetilde\omega_E\). Here \(R_E\) is the Earth radius. Ignore its rotation.
T1-B5 [0.3 pts]. For which station radius \(R\), in terms of \(R_E\), do \(\omega\) and \(\widetilde\omega_E\) coincide?

Alice releases a mass from rest relative to a tower at height \(H\) above the station floor. You can work in an inertial frame or use the station frame, where the Coriolis force is \(\mathbf F_C=2m\mathbf v\times\boldsymbol\omega_{\rm ss}\) with relative velocity \(\mathbf v\). Either sign convention for the horizontal axis is acceptable.

T1-B6 [1.1 pts]. For a short tower the downward acceleration is effectively constant and the sideways displacement is much smaller than \(H\). Find the horizontal speed component \(v_x\) and displacement \(d_x\) relative to the tower base at impact.
T1-B7 [1.3 pts]. From a much taller tower the mass hits its base exactly. Obtain a lower bound on a nonzero tower height permitting this.

Attach the spring to a ring that slides freely along a frictionless horizontal rod parallel to \(x\). The spring oscillates along \(y\), directed towards the centre. Both coordinates lie in the plane perpendicular to the spin axis. The origin is the equilibrium position.

T1-B8 [1.7 pts]. Pull the mass downward by \(d\) and release it from rest. Obtain \(x(t),y(t)\) and sketch the trajectory with its characteristic dimensions. Take \(\omega_{\rm ss}d\) small and neglect the Coriolis contribution to the \(y\) equation.
